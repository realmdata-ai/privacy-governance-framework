# PGF-01 — Privacy Policy and Notice (01A Internal Data Protection Policy · 01B External Privacy Notice)

*Privacy Governance Framework (PGF) · Level 1 — Strategic Documents · Release 1.0.0-rc.1 · Regulatory reference date: 26 October 2026*

> **Status and use.** Part A is a reusable template. Part B is a worked example for a fictional organisation (PanEuropean Bank AG); all names, figures, dates and records in Part B are illustrative. Nothing in this document is legal advice or a statement about any real organisation, product or provider.

# About This Document

Document 1 of the Privacy Governance Framework (PGF) contains two instruments in one component:

* **01A — Internal Data Protection Policy.** The controller's data protection policy within the meaning of GDPR Art. 24(2). It is addressed to staff, managers and the governance bodies. It applies the data-processing principles of GDPR Art. 5, states who decides what, and sets the mandatory gates that every processing decision passes.
* **01B — External Privacy Notice (customers).** The information that the controller gives to customers under GDPR Arts. 12–14, including the information on automated decision-making (Arts. 13(2)(f), 14(2)(g)) and the separate notice of the right to object (Art. 21(4)). Other data-subject domains (employees, candidates, corporate-client contacts, visitors) receive their own notices, which follow the same structure and must not conflict with 01A.

Document 1 is the expression, internal and external, of the principles set in the Privacy Governance Charter (Document 2), which is its parent document. Develop it after the Charter and the Regulatory Applicability Matrix (Document 7) exist at least in draft: the Charter fixes the role model and the gates, and Document 7 fixes which law applies where. The allocation of every process to roles is fixed in the RACI Matrix (Document 5); 01A cites it and does not redefine it.

The template is designed for any large organisation operating across multiple jurisdictions. The GDPR is the legal baseline; the International Adaptation section sets out what changes under UK, Swiss and Canadian law. Industry-specific examples (banking products, KYC/AML) illustrate one possible application and are replaced with the organisation's own.

# How to Use This Document

* **Placeholders.** Bracketed text in bold italics — for example ***[Organisation Legal Name]*** — is replaced with organisation-specific content. Callouts marked **Guidance** explain what belongs in a section and are removed from the adopted policy and the published notice.
* **Numbering.** Sections of 01A are numbered P1–P10. Sections of 01B are numbered 1–16, so that existing cross-references of the form "Document 1, Section 12" (rights), "Section 8" (automated decisions), "Section 11" (retention) or "Section 13" (protective measures) point to the notice.
* **Keep the two audiences apart.** Internal content — governance tiers, risk limits, evidence packs, committee composition, decision rights — belongs in 01A. 01B tells customers what happens to their data, why, on what basis, for how long, who receives it, and how to exercise their rights, in clear and plain language (Art. 12(1)).
* **Layering.** Publish 01B as a layered notice: a short first layer with the key information and the right-to-object box, linked to the full notice (Article 29 Working Party Guidelines on transparency, WP260 rev.01; ISO/IEC 29184:2020 for online notices).
* **Single source of truth.** Lawful bases come from the RoPA (Document 21) and the G-L records; data categories from the Data Classification Policy (Document 10) and the Data Inventory (Document 23); retention from the Data Retention and Disposal Policy (Document 11); jurisdiction-specific rules from Document 7. The notice reproduces them; it does not decide them.

### P3. What We Mean by Privacy

In this Policy, privacy has the meaning fixed in the Charter (Document 2, Section 2): ***[the ability of a data subject to control the collection, use, and dissemination of information about themselves, together with a reasonable expectation that the organisation will process that information in a predictable manner, proportionate to its stated purposes]***. Information security is one means of protecting privacy; it is not the same thing.

The following data states are distinct and are never merged:

* **Personal data** — information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person (Art. 4(1)).
* **Pseudonymised data** — Art. 4(5); it remains personal data for anyone who can attribute it with means reasonably likely to be used. Pseudonymisation is not anonymisation.
* **Anonymous information** — a contextual legal conclusion under Recital 26, reached by the controller on documented evidence (Document 18, PET Assessment Module) and re-assessed periodically.
* **Synthetic data** — a generation method, not a legal status. Generating synthetic data from personal data is itself processing of personal data, and synthetic records may still disclose information about the individuals in the source data; they are tested and their use and disclosure restricted under Document 18, Section 3.3.2.
* **Encrypted data** — a security measure; the data remain personal data.

### P7. Mandatory Gates and Risk Decisions

Every process that leads to a processing, risk or transfer decision applies the gates of the Charter (Document 2, Section 11), in the order fixed in Document 18, Section 2.1: **G-L** (legal compliance gate — lawful basis, Art. 9/10 condition, Art. 6(4) compatibility, transparency deliverable, Art. 22 permissibility, valid transfer tool and TIA outcome, statutory deadlines; failure means stop or redesign) → **G-DPIA** (DPIA triage; DPO advice on every DPIA) → calculation (Document 18) → **G-36** (prior consultation, evaluated for every DPIA as a statutory test) → acceptance by the single decider. **G-TIA** applies to transfers, branched by mechanism (Art. 45 / 46 / 49). A missed statutory deadline is escalated individually.

Residual-risk acceptance (rights-and-freedoms track, after G-L passed):

| Residual tier | Who may accept | Additional mandatory step |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Low | Processing Owner | Record. DPO advice where a DPIA exists. |
| Medium | Processing Owner, with EPPO concurrence | DPO advice recorded. Conditional acceptance carries an expiry date and is re-assessed on expiry. |
| High | One named member of Executive Management, or the Privacy Risk Committee where a written delegation designates it; each decision record names a single effective decider | G-36 evaluated and recorded; prior consultation whenever the Art. 36 test is met; DPO advice recorded; Governing Body committee informed. |
| Critical | Not acceptable | Stop or redesign. |

Organisational consequences (regulatory, financial, reputational) are assessed on a separate track and never offset risk to individuals. Detailed tiers: Document 8, Section 5.

### 8. Automated Decision-Making and Profiling

> **Guidance.** For every decision based solely on automated processing that produces legal or similarly significant effects (Art. 22(1)), state: the Art. 22(2) exception relied on; meaningful information about the logic involved — the main factors and categories of data, how they influence the result, and what is not used; the significance and the envisaged consequences (Arts. 13(2)(f), 14(2)(g)); and the Art. 22(3) safeguards (human intervention, the right to express a point of view and to contest the decision). # International Adaptation

> **Guidance.** The GDPR is the baseline of this document. The box below states what changes in the policy and the notice elsewhere. It does not claim compliance with any jurisdiction's law. Where each regime applies — including the Canadian applicability map — is stated in the Regulatory Applicability Matrix (Document 7, Section 3.1).

**United Kingdom (headline).** UK GDPR and the DPA 2018, as amended by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025, apply with phased commencement; check which provisions have commenced before updating the notice. Headline changes for the notice: the ICO is the regulator; the rules on automated decision-making are recast (permitted with safeguards, with stricter limits for special-category data), so Section 8 cites the UK provisions; individuals may complain to the controller, which must facilitate complaints, before going to the ICO; "recognised legitimate interests" are introduced as a distinct basis; access requests are answered on the basis of reasonable and proportionate searches.

**Switzerland (headline).** Under the revFADP the duty to inform (Art. 19) includes the identity and contact details of the controller, the purposes, the recipients and — for disclosures abroad — the destination countries and the safeguard or exception relied on. Automated individual decisions must be notified, with the right to state one's position and to request review by a natural person (Art. 21). No lawful-basis column is required (the FADP works with justification grounds for specific situations); a Swiss representative is needed where Art. 14 applies; the FDPIC is the authority.

**Canada (PIPEDA and Québec).** A GDPR-style lawful-basis table does not transpose; the policy and notice are organised around purposes, meaningful consent and openness.

* **PIPEDA.** Identify the purposes at or before collection (Principle 4.2) and obtain meaningful consent, appropriate to the sensitivity of the information and the reasonable expectations of the individual (Principle 4.3; OPC *Guidelines for obtaining meaningful consent*). Make specific information about policies and practices readily available (Principle 4.8 — openness), including the name or title and address of the person accountable for compliance (Principle 4.1), how to access personal information, and the types of information held and their use. Access requests are answered within 30 days (Principle 4.9; PIPEDA s. 8). 01A maps to the policies and practices that Principle 4.1.4 requires.
* **Québec (P-39.1).** Publish on the website, in clear and simple terms, detailed information about the organisation's governance policies and practices for personal information, and the title and contact information of the person in charge of the protection of personal information. At collection, inform the person of the purposes, the means of collection, their rights and the possibility that the information may be communicated outside Québec. Where a decision is based exclusively on automated processing, inform the person, and on request tell them the personal information used, the reasons and principal factors that led to the decision, and their right to have the information corrected; give them the opportunity to submit observations to a staff member who can review the decision. Technological functions that allow a person to be identified, located or profiled are disclosed and are off by default. (Topic-level summary; apply the consolidated Act.)

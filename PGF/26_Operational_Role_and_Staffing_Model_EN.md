# PGF-26 — Operational Role and Staffing Model

*Privacy Governance Framework (PGF) · Level 3 — Process Model · Release 1.0.0-rc.2 · Regulatory reference date: 26 October 2026*

> **Status and use.** Part A is a reusable template. Part B is a worked example for a fictional organisation (PanEuropean Bank AG); the named implementation profile identifies a real product; the Bank, deployment, figures, dates and records in Part B are illustrative. Nothing in this document is legal advice or a statement about any real organisation, product or provider.

# **About This Document**

**Purpose.** Document 26 applies the organisational decision (Organisational Design Strategy, Document 4) and the shared models (RACI Matrix, Document 5; Privacy Role Competency Framework, Document 6) to the actual positions and people of the privacy function. For each position it gives a job description — general provisions (reporting lines), qualification requirements, duties, rights, accountability and interactions — and it sets the rules for the register of assignments, vacancies and succession, appointment and removal, and the statutory safeguards of the Data Protection Officer (DPO).

**Scope.** All positions of the privacy function in the indicative headcount (Document 4, Section 5), filled or vacant, including the Executive Privacy Programme Owner (EPPO), the DPO and any deputy DPO. Roles provided by other functions on a shared basis and the Processing Owner mandate (which creates no position) are outside the register of assignments.

**Relationships.** On responsibility allocation and competency content, Documents 5 and 6 prevail; on who actually holds a position today, this document prevails. Processing Owner mandate holders are recorded in the Process Owner Register (Document 25, Section 10).

## **1. Purpose of This Document**

This document applies the organisational decision (Document 4) and the shared models (Documents 5 and 6) to the actual structure and staffing of ***[organisation name]***. It contains a job description for each position — general provisions (reporting lines), qualification requirements, duties, rights, accountability and interactions — and rules for vacancies and succession, appointment and removal, and the DPO's statutory safeguards. Document 4 sets indicative headcount without reference to individuals; Documents 5 and 6 allocate responsibility and qualifications between roles, not people. This document turns them into a form usable for hiring, onboarding and evaluating a specific person in a specific position.

> **Guidance.** On competency content and responsibility allocation, Documents 5 and 6 prevail. On who actually holds a position today, this document is the single source of truth. Updating it for personnel changes does not require revising Documents 4, 5 or 6.

## **2. Scope of Application**

* The document covers all positions of the privacy function in the indicative headcount (Document 4, Section 5) — the EPPO, the DPO and any deputy DPO, the Privacy Operations Lead, Privacy Officers, Privacy Engineers and Data Protection Coordinators — whether filled or vacant.
* It does not cover roles provided by other functions on a shared basis (Document 4, Section 6) — for example a data-protection lawyer in Legal, the ICT risk function for DORA reporting or the AI governance function; they remain in the headcount and management of the providing function and appear here only as interfaces.
* It does not cover the Processing Owner mandate, which is held by business-line heads and creates no position (Section 4.6; Document 25, Section 10).
* It does not override the allocation of responsibility (Document 5) or set qualification requirements (Document 6); it applies both to specific positions and their holders.

## **3. Principle for Building the Model**

* This document is an operational layer beneath Documents 4, 5 and 6: it does not set headcount, allocate responsibility or set qualifications; it records who holds each position and whether the holder meets the requirements.
* Personnel changes (hiring, termination, transfer, temporary cover) do not require revising Documents 4, 5 or 6. Conversely, a change to Documents 4, 5 or 6 — a new role, a changed RACI row, a changed requirement — requires revising the job descriptions here.
* Duties are taken from the rows of Document 5 where the role is Responsible (R) or Accountable (A); rows where the role is only Consulted or Informed appear under interactions. Mandatory consultations (C\*) of the DPO are shown as duties of the DPO, because giving advice is its statutory task, but never as approvals.
* **Segregation of duties.** The same person does not prepare and decide the same matter where the RACI Matrix separates those steps; the DPO is not the EPPO, the Chief Audit Executive or a Processing Owner; a Privacy Officer or Privacy Engineer does not assess the decisions or technical work of a business line in which they also hold a role.
* **Capacity.** Headcount follows the growth triggers of Document 4, Section 5 (for example request volumes, the DPIA queue, countries of presence); this document records the resulting positions.
* Update triggers: appointment to a vacant position; a position becoming vacant; expiry of a qualification confirmation (Document 6, Section 6); a change in the roles of Document 5 or the requirements of Document 6; a change in indicative headcount (Document 4, Section 5); audit findings affecting staffing (Document 27).

## **4. Job Descriptions by Position**

This section is the job description for each position, with one structure: general provisions (reporting lines), qualification requirements, duties, rights, accountability and interactions. Each qualification requirement maps to the competency profile in Document 6, Section 4.

> **Guidance.** Do not re-allocate responsibility here. Group duties by the rows of Document 5 (Sections 4–6) in which the role is R or A; the exact mapping to processes of the map (Document 24) is in Document 5. If a role is neither R nor A for an activity, do not list it as a duty. Mandatory qualifications are vendor-neutral: "a recognised privacy certification or equivalent demonstrable expertise"; named certifications are examples only, and no certificate issued by a tool vendor is a condition for any position.

***4.1 [DPO]***

> **Guidance.** The DPO is an independent statutory role (GDPR Arts. 37–39). Its job description lists only its statutory tasks and the safeguards that protect them. It must not contain programme-management, approval, blocking, risk-acceptance, breach-notification or policy-approval authority, and it must not make the DPO "Accountable" for programme rows. See Section 7.1.

**General Provisions**

* Is designated by ***[Executive Management]*** on the basis of professional qualities and, in particular, expert knowledge of data protection law and practice and the ability to fulfil the tasks of Art. 39 (Art. 37(5)); may be a staff member or act under a service contract (Art. 37(6)); where a group DPO is designated, the DPO is easily accessible from each establishment, including in the languages of the data subjects and supervisory authorities concerned (Art. 37(2))
* Reports directly to the highest management level — ***[Executive Management, administratively through its chair]*** (Art. 38(3)); does not report to the EPPO, the head of data governance, the CISO, a business-line head or any other role whose decisions the DPO advises on or monitors
* Has direct access to the chairs of the ***[Governing Body]***'s risk and audit committees for information where the DPO considers it necessary
* Receives no instructions regarding the exercise of its tasks (Art. 38(3))
* The annual review of the DPO is carried out by ***[the chair of Executive Management]*** and is limited to conduct, availability and use of resources; the content of advice, the outcome of objections and escalations are excluded from any performance or disciplinary assessment (Section 7.1)
* Line-manages only the staff of the DPO office, including any deputy DPO (Section 4.8)

**Qualification Requirements**

* University degree in law, information technology or a related field (Document 6, Section 4.1)
* Mandatory: a recognised privacy-law certification or equivalent demonstrable expertise in data protection law and practice (Art. 37(5)); named certifications are examples only
* At least ***[X]*** years of experience in data protection, compliance or a related regulatory function, including interaction with supervisory authorities
* Knowledge of the sector and of the national law of the establishments served; access to qualified national-law support where the DPO serves several countries
* Understanding of the risk methodology (Document 18) and of automated decision-making and AI sufficient for substantive advice on DPIAs (Document 6, Section 4.8)
* Expert knowledge is maintained with resources provided and funded by the organisation (Art. 38(2)); a lapse of a non-statutory certificate does not suspend the DPO (Section 6)

**Duties**

* Informs and advises the controller, the business lines and staff on their obligations under the GDPR and other data protection law (Art. 39(1)(a)), including mandatory advice (C\*) at G-L, G-DPIA, on every DPIA, on the residual-risk calculation and every acceptance, at G-36, at G-TIA, on policies and notices, on responses to the supervisory authority and on breach notification decisions (Document 5)
* Monitors compliance with data protection law and with the organisation's policies, including the assignment of responsibilities, awareness-raising and training of staff and the related audits it organises (Art. 39(1)(b); Document 5, rows G6.1 and S8.1)
* Provides advice on DPIAs and monitors their performance (Arts. 35(2), 39(1)(c)), whatever the risk tier
* Cooperates with the supervisory authority and acts as its contact point, including on prior consultation (Arts. 36, 39(1)(d)–(e); Document 5, row G4.1(b))
* Handles contacts and complaints from data subjects addressed to the DPO (Art. 38(4)) under the confidentiality duty (Art. 38(5)); decides what, if anything, is shared, using redaction or aggregate reporting where appropriate
* Reports on its own activity directly to Executive Management (Document 5, row G8.2) and informs the Governing Body's committee where it considers it necessary
* Records its advice; where a decision-maker does not follow it, the decision-maker records the reasons and the DPO may escalate directly to Executive Management
* Has due regard to the risk associated with processing operations, taking into account their nature, scope, context and purposes (Art. 39(2))

**Rights**

* To be involved properly and in a timely manner in all issues relating to the protection of personal data (Art. 38(1))
* To the resources necessary to carry out its tasks and maintain its expert knowledge, and to access to personal data and processing operations (Art. 38(2)); an own budget for the DPO office that is not reallocated without the DPO's agreement
* To escalate directly to Executive Management and to inform the Governing Body's committee, whatever the decision taken; escalation does not transfer the decision to the DPO
* Not to be dismissed or penalised for performing its tasks (Art. 38(3)) and to the special protection of national law where it applies (Section 7.1)
* To decide how its own work is distributed and what is disclosed of requests made to it (Art. 38(5))
* To request from any business unit or supporting function the information it needs to perform its tasks

The DPO has no right to approve, authorise or block processing, to accept risk on the controller's behalf, to approve DPIAs, evidence packs, transfers, policies or budgets of the programme, or to decide on breach notification. It holds no veto over a launch.

**Accountability**

* For the quality, timeliness and documentation of its own statutory tasks (Art. 39) — not for the decisions of others
* For confidentiality and secrecy concerning the performance of its tasks (Art. 38(5)) and for the confidentiality of personal data it learns
* For declaring any other task or duty that could result in a conflict of interests (Art. 38(6)), which is assessed case by case

**Interactions**

* ***[Executive Management]*** — direct reporting; annual and ad hoc reports; escalation of advice not followed
* ***[Governing Body committees]*** — information where the DPO considers it necessary
* ***[EPPO]*** — advice on the programme, policies, metrics and reports; the DPO is not part of the EPPO's management line
* ***[Processing Owners]*** — DPIA advice and advice on processing decisions; recorded responses
* ***[Legal]*** — advice on G-L, G-36, transfer-status conclusions and G-TIA
* ***[Privacy Operations Lead, Privacy Officers]*** — information needed for monitoring; Privacy Officers act under the DPO's direction when performing monitoring tasks for the DPO (Document 5, row G6.1)
* ***[Competent supervisory authority]*** — cooperation and contact point (the organisation's responses are prepared by Legal and the EPPO, Document 5, row G4.1(a))
* Data subjects — contacts and complaints addressed to the DPO
* ***[Internal Audit]*** — auditee for its own tasks; input to the risk-based audit plan

***4.2 [Privacy Operations Lead]***

**General Provisions**

* Reports administratively and functionally to ***[the EPPO]*** (Document 4, Section 7)
* Line-manages the Privacy Officer(s) and Privacy Engineer(s); dotted line from the Data Protection Coordinators

**Qualification Requirements**

* University degree; specialisation in data protection, risk management or operations management (Document 6, Section 4.2)
* Mandatory: a recognised privacy programme-management certification or equivalent demonstrable expertise; a recognised privacy-law certification or equivalent within the first year is desirable
* At least ***[X]*** years of experience in the operational management of compliance, risk or quality processes
* Ability to classify a request or project on the standard or non-standard track (Document 5, Section 5.1) and to apply the residual-risk tiers of Document 8 without deciding them

**Duties**

* Prepares, as Responsible, changes to the RACI model, draft policies and standards, the strategy, metrics and programme reports for the EPPO (Document 5, rows G1, G3, G5, G7, G8.1), and the proposal for Processing Owner designations with the entry in Document 25, Section 10 (row G9)
* Is Accountable for the standard track of risk calculation projects: determines whether a configuration is standard, approves projects on the standard track and registers them (row C1); non-standard configurations go to the EPPO and the Privacy Risk Committee
* Is Accountable for intake and triage of processing requests, selection and application of privacy-enhancing techniques, the residual-risk calculation and the evidence pack in core process C2 — without accepting residual risk
* Is Accountable for data subject rights from intake to response (row C3): scope and method of fulfilment; refusals, extensions and restrictions decided on Legal's assessment with DPO advice; every missed statutory deadline escalated
* Is Accountable for identifying transfers in data flows, including remote access by external entities (row C4), and for the storage and proof of consent (row C5)
* Is Accountable for the Data Inventory privacy overlay (row S3), the RoPA (row S4), vendor privacy due diligence (row S6.1) and the operation of the automation tooling (row S9); is the functional owner of the incident management system used for breach records
* Develops the content of the staff training programme with HR, which delivers it (row S8; content adopted by the EPPO)

*As the line manager of the Privacy Officer(s) and Privacy Engineer(s), the Privacy Operations Lead additionally:*

* Allocates tasks and jurisdictional or product workload between direct reports
* Holds regular one-to-one meetings and evaluates the performance of direct reports
* Takes part in selecting candidates for vacant Privacy Officer and Privacy Engineer positions and presents the recommendation to ***[the EPPO]*** (Section 7)
* Agrees the leave schedule of direct reports and organises temporary cover (Section 6)
* Sets development goals for direct reports and monitors mandatory requalification (Document 6, Section 6)
* Ensures that direct reports comply with the internal rules adopted under this model

**Rights**

* Takes the operational decisions assigned to the role in Document 5 (for example the standard track, the residual-risk calculation, DSR responses, the RoPA); has no authority to accept residual risk at any tier
* Approves the choice of privacy-enhancing techniques proposed by the Privacy Engineer(s), within Document 5, row C2
* Initiates regeneration of the Process Owner Register (Document 25) and updates to the RACI model
* Has access to the risk register, the RoPA, the Data Inventory, escalation materials and the automation tooling as needed for the processes it owns
* May request status reporting from direct reports

*Under the line-management branch, the Privacy Operations Lead additionally:*

* Allocates and reallocates workload between direct reports at its discretion
* Submits recommendations to ***[the EPPO]*** on hiring, remuneration or disciplinary measures for direct reports
* Agrees the leave schedule and temporary cover of direct reports without further approval

**Accountability**

* For the outcome of the activities for which the role is Accountable in Document 5, including the accuracy and currency of the RoPA and the Data Inventory privacy overlay
* For timely escalation to the Processing Owner and the EPPO of requests showing signs of High or Critical residual risk, and of missed statutory deadlines
* For the quality of drafts submitted to the EPPO
* For confidentiality of personal data learned in the course of duties

*As a line manager, also:*

* For allocating workload in proportion to the indicative headcount (Document 4)
* For the development and timely requalification of direct reports (Document 6, Section 6)

**Interactions**

* ***[EPPO]*** — reporting line; drafts, escalation, programme decisions
* ***[DPO]*** — mandatory advice on the rows where the DPO is C\*; information for the DPO's monitoring; DPO contacts and complaints passed on only as the DPO decides
* ***[Privacy Officer(s)]***, ***[Privacy Engineer(s)]*** — tasks, reviews; one-to-ones and performance evaluation
* ***[Data Protection Coordinator(s)]*** — dotted-line methodological support; escalations from business units
* ***[Legal]*** — G-L, refusals and restrictions, TIAs
* ***[Processing Owners]*** — DPIA ownership and decisions; notification of designations and of changes to the mandate
* Business units, IT — fulfilment of requests, retention execution, consent implementation
* ***[HR]*** — training delivery; recommendations on hiring, remuneration and disciplinary measures for direct reports

***4.3 [Privacy Officer]***

**General Provisions**

* Reports administratively to ***[the Privacy Operations Lead]*** (in an initial phase without that position — to ***[the EPPO]***, Document 4, Section 9)
* Escalates to ***[the EPPO]*** matters that require escalation under the conflict-resolution matrix (Document 3, Section 5)
* When performing monitoring tasks for the DPO, acts under the DPO's direction (Document 5, row G6.1)

**Qualification Requirements**

* University degree; legal, compliance or related (Document 6, Section 4.3)
* Mandatory within ***[6]*** months of appointment: a recognised privacy-law certification or equivalent demonstrable expertise
* ***[X]*** years of experience in data protection or compliance, or a completed internal onboarding programme
* Working knowledge of the data protection law, supervisory practice and official language of the country served
* Working proficiency in the DPIA methodology (Document 19)

**Duties**

* Performs regulatory monitoring for its jurisdiction and passes changes to ***[the Privacy Operations Lead]*** (Document 5, row G4)
* Drafts privacy notices and transparency content with Legal (row G5.1)
* Assesses public-authority access requests with Legal, which decides (row G4.2)
* Performs intake and triage of processing requests, with the business unit (row C2)
* Conducts DPIAs with the business unit and the CISO for the Processing Owner, who owns the DPIA; compiles the substantive part of the evidence pack with the Privacy Engineer(s)
* Supports the TIA and the mechanism check performed by Legal (row C4)
* Performs intake and identity verification of data subject requests; where identity cannot be verified, requests additional information (Art. 12(6)) rather than refusing to accept the request; assesses scope and method with ***[the Privacy Operations Lead]***; checks fulfilment by the business unit or IT; drafts the response; refers a proposed refusal to the Privacy Operations Lead before any response is sent
* Maintains RoPA records (row S4) and performs vendor privacy due diligence with Procurement (row S6.1)
* Qualifies and records every personal data breach (row S7.2) and prepares processor notifications to client controllers (row S7.5)
* Supports children's-data safeguards (row C2.2), legitimate-interest assessments (row C2.3) and the privacy workstream of corporate transactions (row S6.3)

**Rights**

* Prepares analyses and recommendations; has no right to decide on residual risk
* May request from business units and IT the information needed for DPIAs, requests and records
* Has access to the RoPA, due-diligence materials and DPIA files within its jurisdiction or area

**Accountability**

* For the completeness and accuracy of the substantive part of DPIAs, evidence packs and RoPA records it prepares
* For handling data subject requests within the statutory deadlines of the applicable law and for timely escalation of disputed or complex requests
* For confidentiality of personal data learned in the course of duties

**Interactions**

* ***[Privacy Operations Lead]*** — reporting line; escalation; scope of requests
* Business units in its jurisdiction — intake and DPIAs
* ***[Data Protection Coordinator(s)]*** — escalations from the business; advice
* ***[Privacy Engineer(s)]*** — evidence packs; technical part of DPIAs
* ***[Legal]*** — G-L, TIAs, refusals, authority requests
* ***[DPO]*** — direction when performing monitoring tasks for the DPO; mandatory advice on DPIAs
* Procurement — vendor due diligence
* Data subjects — requests and responses

***4.4 [Privacy Engineer]***

**General Provisions**

* Reports administratively to ***[the Privacy Operations Lead]***; escalates to ***[the EPPO]*** (Document 4, Section 7)
* Has a dotted line to ***[IT Management]*** on infrastructure for the automation tooling — the only formalised dotted line outside the privacy function (Document 3, Section 3.6)

**Qualification Requirements**

* Mandatory: a recognised privacy-engineering certification or equivalent demonstrable expertise (for example an assessed portfolio of privacy-engineering work)
* Internal proficiency assessment on the organisation's automation tooling and its documented limitations, before independent authorisation to configure privacy-enhancing techniques; no vendor-issued certificate is required
* ***[X]*** years of practical data-engineering experience, including pseudonymisation and synthetic data

**Duties**

* Prepares risk calculation project charters and determines, with ***[the Privacy Operations Lead]***, whether the configuration is standard (row C1)

* Compiles the technical part of the evidence pack with the Privacy Officer(s)
* Identifies transfers in data flows, including remote access by external entities (row C4)
* Implements the technical storage and proof of consent; the content of consent is decided by the Processing Owner (row C5)
* Operates the automation tooling (row S9); infrastructure support is provided by IT

**Rights**

* Proposes techniques for approval by ***[the Privacy Operations Lead]***; has no right to decide on residual risk
* Has technical access to the tooling and data flows needed to configure techniques
* May request infrastructure support from IT

**Accountability**

* For the technical correctness of the techniques applied and the completeness of the technical documentation in the evidence pack
* For the timely identification of transfers in data flows
* For confidentiality of personal data learned in the course of duties

**Interactions**

* ***[Privacy Operations Lead]*** — approval of techniques; functional requirements for the tooling
* ***[Privacy Officer(s)]*** — evidence packs; technical part of DPIAs and TIAs
* ***[IT Management / CISO]*** — technical implementation and infrastructure
* ***[External technical support provider, if any]*** — configuration questions and known limitations of the tooling, under the provider's contract (Document 17)

***4.5 [Data Protection Coordinator]***

> **Guidance.** The Data Protection Coordinator (DPC) is defined as in the RACI Matrix (Document 5, Section 3): an execution-tier, distributed role within a business unit. It has no rows of its own in the matrix and no decision authority; it is a communication channel between the business unit and the privacy function (Document 6, Section 4.5). Where the abbreviation could be confused with a supervisory authority's name, spell the role out.

**General Provisions**

* Reports administratively to the head of its business unit (solid line); functionally to ***[the Privacy Operations Lead]*** (dotted line) (Document 4, Section 7)
* Holds the role concurrently with a primary position in the unit

**Qualification Requirements**

* Not set separately from the primary position
* Internal certification under the training programme (Document 30); external certification is not required

**Duties**

* Recognises situations requiring the privacy function — new or changed processing, a possible personal data breach, a request from a data subject — and escalates them to ***[the Privacy Operations Lead]***; never decides on the permissibility of processing
* Completes the annual training (Document 6, Section 6)

**Rights**

* May ask ***[the Privacy Operations Lead]*** for advice on any doubtful matter
* Has no right to decide on the permissibility of processing or on residual risk

**Accountability**

* For timely recognition and escalation
* For completing the annual training
* For confidentiality of information learned in the course of duties

**Interactions**

* Head of the business unit — solid line; priorities in the primary position
* ***[Privacy Operations Lead]*** — dotted line; escalation; methodological support
* Colleagues in the unit — awareness of privacy requirements
* ***[Processing Owner]*** of the unit — information on designations and changes of the mandate

***4.6 Processing Owner — Mandate, Not a Position***

The Processing Owner is a mandate held by a business-line head (or the head of IT for IT-operated processing), designated by ***[Executive Management]*** on the EPPO's proposal (Document 5, row G9). It creates no position in the privacy function, is not part of the indicative headcount (Document 4, Section 5) and is not recorded in the register of assignments (Section 5). The role profile is in Document 6, Section 4.6; the holders are recorded in Document 25, Section 10.

***4.7 [EPPO]***

**General Provisions**

* Is designated by ***[Executive Management]***; reports to ***[a member of Executive Management]*** (Document 4, Section 7)
* May hold another executive post (for example CPO or head of privacy); no separate new post is required; may not be the DPO or the Chief Audit Executive
* Line-manages the Privacy Operations Lead and, through that role, the privacy operations team

**Qualification Requirements**

* University degree in law, risk management or a related field (Document 6, Section 4.7)
* Mandatory: a recognised privacy programme-management certification or equivalent demonstrable expertise, and working knowledge of the data protection law applicable to the organisation
* At least ***[X]*** years in compliance, risk or privacy, including leadership of a cross-functional programme reported to executive management
* Knowledge of the mandatory gates, the two-track risk model (Document 18) and the separation of GDPR breach notification from sector-specific incident reporting (Document 14)

**Duties**

* Is Accountable for operating the privacy programme (Document 5): strategy (G1), risk limits and methodology (G2.1, G2.2), the RACI model (G3), regulatory monitoring (G4), the organisation's responses to supervisory-authority inquiries (G4.1(a)), policies and standards (G5) and the notice framework (G5.1), metrics (G7), programme reporting (G8.1), Processing Owner designation proposals (G9), the retention schedule (S2.1), the privacy risk register (S5), breach qualification and recording (S7.2) and training (S8)
* Is Accountable for the non-standard track of risk calculation projects and for new privacy-enhancing techniques (rows C1, C2), with the Privacy Risk Committee deciding where Document 5 so provides
* Gives concurrence to Medium residual-risk acceptance by Processing Owners (Document 8); prepares prior consultations with the supervisory authority with the Processing Owner (G-36)
* Prepares and files GDPR breach notifications decided by the designated member of Executive Management (row S7.3)
* Leads the privacy workstream of corporate transactions (row S6.3)
* Ensures the DPO's resources, access and independence on behalf of Executive Management and prepares DPO designation and contact-detail communication (statutory rows)

**Rights**

* Takes the programme decisions assigned in Document 5; gives or withholds concurrence at the Medium tier
* Has no authority to accept High residual risk; may not instruct the DPO on the exercise of its tasks
* Has access to programme records; receives DPO material only as the DPO decides (Art. 38(5))
* Appoints and removes holders of the positions in its line (Privacy Operations Lead, Privacy Officers, Privacy Engineers) with HR (Section 7)

**Accountability**

* For the functioning of the programme and the accuracy of programme reporting to Executive Management and the Governing Body
* For applying Document 8 consistently in its concurrence decisions
* For confidentiality of personal data learned in the course of duties

**Interactions**

* ***[Executive Management]*** and its designated member — reporting line; adoption of policies; High-tier decisions; breach notification decisions
* ***[Governing Body committees]*** — programme reports
* ***[DPO]*** — advice; the DPO is outside the EPPO's line
* ***[Privacy Risk Committee]*** — chair or member, as the organisation's design provides
* ***[Processing Owners]*** — concurrence; designations
* ***[Legal]*** — G-L, G-36, G-TIA, supervisory-authority responses
* ***[Privacy Operations Lead]*** — reporting line; programme operations

***7.1 Statutory Safeguards for the DPO***

> **Guidance.** These safeguards follow GDPR Arts. 37–39, WP243 rev.01 and the case law cited; add the national rules of the jurisdictions concerned (for a mandatory DPO in Germany, for example, §38(2) with §6(4) BDSG). They apply equally to any deputy DPO.

* **Designation and publication.** The DPO is designated on the basis of professional qualities and expert knowledge (Art. 37(5)); the contact details are published and communicated to the supervisory authority (Art. 37(7)). A group DPO must be easily accessible from each establishment (Art. 37(2)), including in the languages needed.
* **Involvement and resources.** The organisation involves the DPO properly and in a timely manner (Art. 38(1)) and provides the resources necessary to carry out its tasks, access to personal data and processing operations, and to maintain its expert knowledge (Art. 38(2)). The organisation funds the DPO's certification upkeep and continuing professional education.
* **No instructions; no penalty.** The DPO receives no instructions on the exercise of its tasks, is not dismissed or penalised for performing them, and reports directly to the highest management level (Art. 38(3)). National law may provide stronger protection against dismissal, which applies in addition (for a mandatory DPO in Germany: §38(2) with §6(4) BDSG; CJEU C-534/20 *Leistritz*).
* **Performance assessment.** The DPO's review is carried out by the highest management level and is limited to conduct, availability and use of resources. The content of the DPO's advice, the fact or outcome of objections, and escalations are excluded from any performance, remuneration or disciplinary assessment. Effectiveness indicators for the DPO cover only the timeliness, coverage and documentation of its statutory tasks (Document 28).
* **Confidentiality.** The DPO is bound by secrecy or confidentiality concerning the performance of its tasks (Art. 38(5)). "Consulted" or "Informed" roles in Document 5 never imply automatic disclosure of the content of a request made to the DPO or of the requester's identity; disclosure follows need-to-know, purpose limitation and confidentiality, with redaction or aggregate reporting where appropriate (WP243 rev.01, §2.6). The DPO decides how its own work is distributed; this does not prevent it from investigating matters or discussing them with management.
* **Conflicts of interest.** Other tasks and duties of the DPO must not result in a conflict of interests (Art. 38(6)). The organisation records an assessment on designation and reviews it annually, case by case (CJEU C-453/21 *X-FAB*; EDPB Coordinated Enforcement Action 2023 report). Positions that determine purposes and means — for example heads of business lines, the EPPO, the head of data governance, the CISO — are not combined with the DPO role.
* **Measures concerning the DPO.** Any disciplinary or personnel measure concerning the DPO or a deputy DPO is decided by ***[Executive Management]***, not by an administrative superior alone, after the person has been heard; where the DPO considers that a measure relates to the performance of its tasks, the chair of the ***[Governing Body]***'s competent committee is informed. Measures on grounds unrelated to the performance of the DPO's tasks are not barred.

When a position becomes vacant, the register is updated at once and the Privacy Operations Lead sets temporary cover under Section 6. IT Management revokes access to PEP, the "Conduct & Privacy Risk" register, the RoPA and other privacy systems no later than the employee's last working day, on notice from the Privacy Operations Lead or PanEuropean HR. Handover is documented and covers open DPIAs, customer requests and transfers assigned to the position; handover in the DPO office respects the confidentiality of requests made to the Group DPO.

***7.1 Statutory Safeguards for the Group DPO***

The safeguards of Part A, Section 7.1 apply to the Group DPO and the Deputy Group DPO, with these PanEuropean specifics:

* **Designation and publication.** Designated by the Management Board; contact details (dpo@paneuropean-bank.example) published in the Customer Privacy Notice and communicated to the HBDI (Art. 37(7)); accessibility from the five countries ensured by the Deputy Group DPO's languages and local-language channels (Art. 37(2)).
* **Resources.** The DPO office budget (Document 4, Section 8) covers the Group DPO's and the Deputy's salaries, expert knowledge, certification upkeep, legal literature and external advice, and is not reallocated without the Group DPO's agreement (Art. 38(2)).
* **No instructions; no penalty; dismissal protection.** Art. 38(3) applies; as a mandatory DPO in Germany, the special protection of §38(2) with §6(4) BDSG applies to the Group DPO's dismissal and to the termination of the employment relationship (CJEU C-534/20 *Leistritz*; C-453/21 *X-FAB*).
* **Performance assessment.** By the Chair of the Management Board, limited to conduct, availability and use of resources; indicators only as in Document 28 (timeliness, coverage, documentation). The content of advice, objections later shown to be unfounded and escalations are excluded from any performance, remuneration or disciplinary assessment.
* **Confidentiality.** Contacts and complaints to the Group DPO are handled under Art. 38(5); the CPO and the Privacy Operations Lead receive aggregate or redacted information unless the Group DPO decides otherwise (Document 5, Section 5.3).
* **Conflicts of interest.** Recorded on designation and reviewed annually; the Group DPO holds no position in Data Governance, Digital Banking, Marketing or any other unit that determines purposes and means, and does not report to the CDO.
* **Measures concerning the Group DPO.** Decided by the Management Board, not by an administrative superior alone, after the person has been heard; where the Group DPO considers that a measure relates to the performance of its tasks, the chair of the Risk Committee of the Supervisory Board is informed.

# **International Adaptation**

> **Guidance.** The GDPR is the baseline. The applicability map for Canada is in Document 7, Section 3.1.

* **United Kingdom (headline).** UK GDPR Arts. 37–39 contain the same DPO regime; the competent authority is the ICO.
* **Switzerland (headline).** A data protection advisor is optional for private controllers (revFADP Art. 10); where one is appointed and meets the conditions of independence, expertise and no incompatible tasks, the controller may consult the advisor instead of the FDPIC after a high-risk impact assessment. Use the DPO job description with those conditions.
* **Canada — PIPEDA.** The individual accountable for compliance under Principle 4.1 (often called the Privacy Officer) is typically the EPPO in this model and may hold programme accountability; that person is not an EU DPO, and the DPO's independence safeguards are not transferred automatically. Job descriptions refer to PIPEDA breach duties (real risk of significant harm; records of every breach of security safeguards kept for 24 months) and access requests (30 days).
* **Canada — Québec (P-39.1).** The person exercising the highest authority is the person in charge of the protection of personal information by default and may delegate the function in writing, in whole or in part; record the delegation and the person's title and contact information, which are published on the organisation's website.

# PGF-27 — Privacy Audit and Monitoring Programme

*Privacy Governance Framework (PGF) · Level 4 — Control, Reporting and Improvement · Release 1.0.0-rc.1 · Regulatory reference date: 26 October 2026*

> **Status and use.** Part A is a reusable template. Part B is a worked example for a fictional organisation (PanEuropean Bank AG); all names, figures, dates and records in Part B are illustrative. Nothing in this document is legal advice or a statement about any real organisation, product or provider.

# **About This Document**

**Purpose.** Document 27 sets out how the organisation checks that the privacy model of the PGF is followed in practice, not only adopted on paper. It separates three loops that are often confused: continuous first- and second-line monitoring by management; the DPO's monitoring of compliance, including the related audits the DPO organises (GDPR Art. 39(1)(b)); and the independent assurance opinion of Internal Audit, which reports to the Governing Body's Audit Committee.

**Scope.** All documents and processes of the framework, the records they produce, and the counterparties that process personal data for or with the organisation, to the extent of contractual audit rights.

**Relationships.** Roles follow the RACI Matrix (Document 5, rows G6.1, G6.2a, G6.2b) and the "Compliance Monitoring and Assurance" process (Document 24, Section 4.7). Audit and monitoring metrics are defined in the Privacy Metrics Dashboard (Document 28, Section 6.5). Escalation addressees and time limits follow the master escalation matrix (Document 31, Section 6.4). Review of the content of the model is the Policy Review Cycle (Document 29).

## **1. Purpose of the Document**

This document is Document 27 of the Privacy Governance Framework (PGF), the first document of Level 4 "Control, Reporting and Improvement". It sets out how compliance with the adopted privacy model is monitored and how independent assurance on it is obtained: the audit universe, the verification criteria per framework level, the schedule and method of each loop, the classification and escalation of non-conformities, and the tracking of remediation to closure.

Three loops are distinguished:

| Loop | Who | Nature | Reports to |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Continuous monitoring (first and second line) | ***[Process owners; EPPO; Privacy Operations Lead]*** | Key indicators (Document 28), control self-assessment, quality checks | ***[EPPO; Executive Management through the programme report (Document 31)]*** |
| Compliance monitoring and related audits (G6.1) | ***[DPO]***, with staff acting under the DPO's direction | Statutory task of the DPO (Art. 39(1)(b)): monitoring compliance with the GDPR, other data protection law and the controller's policies, including assignment of responsibilities, awareness-raising and training, and the related audits | ***[Executive Management]***; the ***[Audit Committee]*** where the DPO considers it necessary |
| Independent assurance (G6.2a, G6.2b) | ***[Independent Internal Audit]***, led by the ***[Chief Audit Executive]***; external specialists engaged by it | Third-line assurance under the internal-audit charter and a risk-based plan approved by the Audit Committee | ***[Audit Committee]***; ***[Executive Management]*** informed |

The DPO's monitoring and Internal Audit's assurance complement each other. The DPO organises monitoring and audits as part of its statutory tasks and is responsible and accountable for them; it does not issue the final independent assurance opinion on a programme whose operation it advises on and monitors. Internal Audit issues that opinion and also covers the DPO function itself (resources, independence, conflicts of interest).

Unlike the Policy Review Cycle (Document 29), which asks whether the model is still right, this document asks whether the adopted model is actually followed (Section 11).

> **Guidance.** The programme does not create a new register of privacy facts. It tests the records already kept — RoPA (Document 21), Privacy Risk Register (Document 22), breach record (Document 14), rights-request log (Document 15), consent records (Document 13) — and keeps one issues log for findings (Section 10).

## **3. Principles**

* **Independence and objectivity.** Internal Audit is independent of the programme and of the DPO; it reports functionally to the Audit Committee. The DPO performs its monitoring without instructions on how to exercise its tasks (Art. 38(3)), but it does not issue assurance on its own tasks, and no one audits work they performed or decided in the same cycle (Section 8)
* **Risk orientation.** The frequency and depth of review are proportionate to the residual risk of the process and its processing activities (Document 8); High-tier activities, automated decisions, special categories, transfers and incident handling are covered more often and more deeply
* **Evidence basis.** Findings rely on verifiable records — DPIA, evidence pack, RoPA entry, register record, breach record, rights-request log — not on verbal assurances
* **Methodological adequacy.** The test covers not only that a DPIA or decision exists, but that the methodology was applied correctly: that the likelihood and severity classification follows Document 18, that the decider was the one designated under Document 8, that G-L ran before any risk decision and G-36 was evaluated and recorded, and that PET-module results are recorded per metric and gate — including the criteria of singling out, linkability and inference from Article 29 Working Party Opinion 05/2014 on Anonymisation Techniques (WP216) — rather than averaged
* **Adversarial re-validation (optional technique).** For released de-identified or synthetic artefacts, the monitoring or audit plan may include an adversarial re-validation: a documented, controlled attempt to single out, link or infer information about individuals from the released artefact, performed by persons not involved in producing it. A successful attack is a finding and triggers re-assessment under Document 18 and, where personal data may have been disclosed, Document 14. A failed attack is evidence, not proof of anonymity
* **Remediation, not fault-finding.** A non-conformity is an input for correction, policy review (Document 29), training (Document 30) or tooling (Document 9); it is not by itself a disciplinary matter
* **Compliance issues are not risks to accept.** G-L failures and missed statutory deadlines are recorded in the issues log with an owner and a remediation date, never as accepted risks (Document 22)
* **Confidentiality.** Monitoring by the DPO respects the confidentiality of requests made to it (Art. 38(5)); results that would disclose a requester's identity are reported in aggregate or redacted

## **6. Schedule and Methodology**

### **6.1 DPO Compliance Monitoring and Related Audits (G6.1)**

* The DPO sets its own risk-based monitoring plan at least ***[annually]***, taking into account the risks associated with processing operations (Art. 39(2)); management may propose topics but does not instruct the DPO on how to exercise its tasks (Art. 38(3))
* The plan may cover any part of the audit universe (Section 2) — for example, data subject request handling, consent proof, breach records, DPIA records and the allocation of responsibilities — and the awareness-raising and training of staff (Art. 39(1)(b))
* Techniques: walkthroughs, sampling, re-performance, data analysis, interviews and, where useful, adversarial re-validation of released PET artefacts (Section 3)
* Staff acting under the DPO's direction for monitoring do not monitor processes they themselves operate
* The DPO reports results to ***[Executive Management]*** and, where it considers it necessary, to the ***[Audit Committee]***; findings enter the issues log (Section 10)
* The DPO receives the resources necessary for the plan (Art. 38(2)); plan completion is shown as metric M-G6-05 (Document 28) as a resource signal, not a performance measure

### **6.2 Internal-Audit Charter, Risk-Based Plan and Engagements (G6.2a, G6.2b)**

* The ***[Chief Audit Executive]*** drafts and executes the internal-audit charter, the risk-based audit plan — including privacy engagements — and significant changes to the plan; the ***[Audit Committee]*** approves them (IIA Global Internal Audit Standards 2024, Standards 6.2 and 9.4). Executive Management, the EPPO and the DPO are consulted on the risk assessment, not on the conclusions
* Coverage: ***[processes at High residual risk, automated decision-making, special categories, transfers and incident handling at least annually; the full audit universe at least every X years]***
* For financial entities, the ICT risk-management framework is subject to internal audit on a regular basis by auditors with sufficient knowledge, skills and expertise in ICT risk and appropriate independence (DORA Art. 6(6)); privacy engagements are coordinated with that cycle
* External specialists (for example, for PET topics) are engaged and directed by the Chief Audit Executive; the auditees do not select, contract or oversee them
* The independent opinion is issued by the Chief Audit Executive to the Audit Committee, with Executive Management informed. It uses the rating scale of the internal-audit charter ***[e.g. effective / needs improvement / ineffective]***

### **6.3 Continuous Monitoring (First and Second Line)**

* Process owners run control self-assessments at least ***[semi-annually]*** against the criteria of Section 5 for their processes
* The EPPO and the Privacy Operations Lead monitor the key indicators of Document 28 and act on deviations under Document 28, Section 10
* Results are inputs to the DPO's monitoring plan and to Internal Audit's risk assessment; they do not replace either

### **6.4 Unscheduled Reviews**

An unscheduled review by the DPO, or an unscheduled engagement by Internal Audit within its plan-change rules, is considered on: ***[a personal data breach (Document 14); a material complaint or supervisory-authority inquiry; a missed statutory deadline; a material change of processing, organisation (Document 4) or law (Document 7); a Critical item under Document 28, Section 10; a finding of a financial supervisor with a privacy dimension]***.

### **6.5 Engagement Method**

> **Guidance.** The steps mirror the "Compliance Monitoring and Assurance" process (Document 24, Section 4.7). Do not introduce a sequence incompatible with the process map.

* Planning — scope, criteria (Section 5), risk profile (Document 8), results of earlier cycles and of the other loops
* Sampling — risk-based; the sample size and rationale are documented in the engagement plan, with full coverage of High-tier decisions where feasible
* Fieldwork — evidence gathering, interviews, re-performance, reconciliation of records
* Preliminary findings — sent to the process owner for a right of reply within ***[X]*** business days
* Final conclusion — conformity or non-conformity per criterion, with the owner's position where it differs
* Follow-up — closure is verified on evidence; Internal Audit performs follow-up engagements on its own findings

### **6.6 Reconciling the Loops**

* Results of continuous monitoring and of the DPO's monitoring feed Internal Audit's risk assessment and engagement planning; Internal Audit's findings feed the DPO's next plan
* Where findings of two loops diverge, the divergence is documented and analysed; it is not resolved by default in favour of either side. Internal Audit's opinion is the organisation's independent assurance; it does not overrule the DPO's statutory advice, and the DPO's monitoring does not overrule the audit opinion

## **7. Non-Conformity Classification and Escalation**

The levels below classify a deviation of a control from an adopted rule. They share the names of the residual-risk tiers (Document 8, Section 4) but are a different scale. Addressees and time limits follow the master escalation matrix (Document 31, Section 6.4), under which a higher level always has a shorter time limit.

| Level | Description | Examples |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Low | ***[A local deviation from procedure without material risk]*** | ***[A one-off delay of 1–2 days in updating a RoPA entry]*** |
| Medium | ***[A systematic deviation, or a deviation with material but limited potential impact]*** | ***[A discrepancy between the Process Owner Register and the RACI Matrix for one process]*** |
| High | ***[A deviation that creates material risk to individuals or breaches a legal duty in a single case]*** | ***[A DPIA decided without the DPO's recorded advice or without a G-36 conclusion; a rights request refused without a documented ground; a decision taken by a person not designated; any missed statutory deadline]*** |
| Critical | ***[An actual infringement affecting many individuals or a statutory step; loss of DPO independence; signs of a breach]*** | ***[Processing started before a required prior consultation step; systematic missed statutory deadlines; the DPO instructed on how to perform its tasks]*** |

* A G-L failure or a missed statutory deadline is at least High, is recorded in the issues log (Section 10) and is never treated as an accepted risk
* A non-conformity indicating a possible personal data breach activates the Data Breach Response Policy (Document 14) in parallel; this document never delays its clocks
* A recurring non-conformity in the same process and criterion is raised one level (Section 10)

## **8. Independence and Objectivity**

* Internal Audit reports functionally to the ***[Audit Committee]*** and administratively to ***[the Chief Executive / Executive Management]***; the internal-audit charter protects its access to records and people
* An external firm engaged for privacy engagements does not provide privacy or data protection advisory services within the audit scope during the engagement and for ***[X years]*** before it; the lead engagement partner rotates at least every ***[X years]***
* No auditor or monitoring staff member reviews a process in whose execution or decisions they took part in the same cycle
* The DPO's independence, resources (Art. 38(2)), reporting line (Art. 38(3)) and absence of conflicts of interest (Art. 38(6); CJEU C-453/21 *X-FAB*) are assessed by Internal Audit on substance, not on the formal reporting line, and never by the DPO itself
* The EPPO and process owners, as auditees, do not approve audit scope, select auditors or edit conclusions

## **10. Conclusions, Issues Log and Remediation Tracking**

* Before a conclusion is final, the process owner may reply in writing within the period of the plan; the conclusion records any divergent position
* Each conclusion contains: the criteria tested; confirmed conformity; non-conformities with their level (Section 7); for Internal Audit, the overall rating; the remediation plan with owner and date
* **Issues log.** One log, kept in ***[the GRC platform]***, records: audit and monitoring findings; G-L failures; missed statutory deadlines; and other compliance issues — each with owner, remediation date, status and evidence of closure. An item is closed only on evidence, not on the owner's statement
* A non-conformity that recurs in the next cycle for the same process and criterion is raised one level, because the cause was not removed
* Recipients: Internal Audit's opinion goes to the ***[Audit Committee]***, with Executive Management informed; the DPO's monitoring results go to ***[Executive Management]*** and, where the DPO considers it necessary, to the Audit Committee; the programme report (Document 31) summarises the status of both and of the issues log
* Indicators of this programme — coverage, plan completion, remediation on time, recurring non-conformities, items past due — are metrics M-G6-01 to M-G6-07 of Document 28 (Section 6.5)

* Risk orientation: credit scoring (automated credit decisions, PRR-CS-INFER-01 and PRR-CS-LAUNCH-01 at High), the customer-service assistant and its US transfer (TIA-LLM-01), the PSI anti-fraud matching (TIA-PSI-01), the federated-learning consortium and breach handling are covered every year; the administrative tasks of the Data Protection Coordinators are covered by a reduced programme
* Evidence basis: findings rely on PEP evidence packs, RoPA entries for the five countries, the Privacy Risk Register (the "Conduct & Privacy Risk" extension of the enterprise risk register) and the breach and rights-request records
* Methodological adequacy: for example, whether the synthetic-data release for DPIA-CS-TRAIN-01 recorded each PET-module metric and failure gate individually, including the singling-out, linkability and inference criteria of WP29 Opinion 05/2014 (WP216), and whether the first generator run (which failed the module) remains charged to the disclosure-budget ledger
* Adversarial re-validation (optional technique): the Group DPO's 2027 monitoring plan includes an adversarial re-validation of the open statistics release of Document 18, Example 3.1, by Privacy Engineers not involved in producing it, under a documented test plan (planned, Q1 2027)
* Remediation, not fault-finding: findings feed correction, policy review (Document 29), training (Document 30) or PEP changes (Document 9)
* Compliance issues are recorded in the issues log, never as accepted risks

## **6. Schedule and Methodology**

***6.1 Group DPO Monitoring (G6.1)***

* The Group DPO sets an annual, risk-based monitoring plan; the 2027 plan is due in January 2027 (planned). In Q3 2026 the Group DPO performed four monitoring activities: a sample of data subject requests, consent proof in the Consent Manager, RoPA entries against the Process Owner Register, and DPIA records
* The Group DPO may ask Privacy Officers to perform monitoring steps under its direction; a Privacy Officer does not monitor requests or DPIAs it handled itself
* Results go to the Management Board (Chief Executive as Chair, and the Chief Risk Officer) and, where the Group DPO considers it necessary, to the Audit Committee

***6.2 Group Internal Audit (G6.2a, G6.2b)***

* The Chief Audit Executive drafts the internal-audit charter and the risk-based annual plan; the Audit Committee approves them. The 2026 plan, approved by the Audit Committee in December 2025 (illustrative), includes a privacy engagement in Q4 2026. An updated scope reflecting the new role model is a significant plan change, submitted for the Audit Committee's approval on 16 November 2026 (planned)
* Cycle: credit scoring, transfers (C4) and incident and breach management (S7) every year; the other processes at least every three years; the Group DPO monitoring loop and the DPO function (independence, resources) at least every three years
* Under DORA Art. 6(6), PanEuropean's ICT risk-management framework is audited regularly by Group Internal Audit; privacy engagements touching ICT controls are coordinated with that cycle
* Nordbrücke Audit & Assurance GmbH is engaged by the Chief Audit Executive for specialist PET topics (DP accounting, synthetic-data validation, PSI protocol review); Procurement administers the contract on the Chief Audit Executive's instruction. Neither the Group DPO nor the Chief Privacy Officer oversees the contract or approves the scope
* The opinion uses Group Internal Audit's rating scale (effective / needs improvement / ineffective) and goes to the Audit Committee, with the Management Board informed

***6.3 Continuous Monitoring***

Process owners perform a control self-assessment every six months; the Chief Privacy Officer and the Privacy Operations Lead monitor the dashboard (Document 28) weekly.

***6.4 Unscheduled Reviews***

Considered on: a personal data breach (Document 14); a complaint about a refused Request a Review; an HBDI or concerned-authority inquiry; a missed statutory deadline; entry into a new country; a finding of the ECB or BaFin with a privacy dimension; a material change of law recorded in Document 7.

***6.5 Engagement Method***

* Sampling: all High-tier decisions and all DPIAs for credit scoring and anti-fraud; at least 25% of RoPA entries and DPIAs for other processes, selected by residual tier, with the rationale recorded in the engagement plan
* Fieldwork: interviews with the Privacy Officers of the five countries, the Privacy Engineers and the Data Protection Coordinators; evidence extracted directly from PEP and the source registers
* Preliminary findings within 4 weeks of fieldwork; right of reply within 5 business days
* Follow-up by Group Internal Audit on its own findings; by the Group DPO on its monitoring findings

***6.6 Reconciling the Loops***

Group DPO monitoring results and control self-assessments feed Group Internal Audit's risk assessment; divergences are documented. The independent opinion does not overrule the Group DPO's statutory advice, and the Group DPO's monitoring does not overrule the opinion.

## **7. Non-Conformity Classification and Escalation**

Addressees and time limits follow Document 31, Part B, Section 6.4: Medium — Privacy Operations Lead, remediation plan within 10 business days; High — Chief Privacy Officer within 2 business days, plan within 5 business days; Critical — Chief Privacy Officer and Chief Risk Officer the same business day, Management Board measures within 5 business days, chair of the Audit Committee (audit findings) or of the Risk Committee informed within 2 business days; a missed statutory deadline — Chief Privacy Officer and General Counsel within 1 business day, Group DPO informed.

| Level | Example at PanEuropean |
| :---- | :---- |
| Low | A one-off delay of 1–2 days in updating a RoPA entry |
| Medium | A discrepancy between the Process Owner Register and the RACI Matrix for one process |
| High | A DPIA for a new scoring feature decided without the Group DPO's recorded advice or without a G-36 conclusion; a Request a Review refused without a documented ground; an access request answered after the statutory deadline |
| Critical | Automated credit decisions put into production before the HBDI consultation step and the Chief Risk Officer's decision; repeated missed statutory deadlines with a common cause; an instruction to the Group DPO on how to perform its tasks |

A Critical non-conformity indicating a possible breach activates Document 14 in parallel.

## **8. Independence and Objectivity**

* Group Internal Audit reports functionally to the Audit Committee and administratively to the Chief Executive
* Nordbrücke provides no privacy or data protection advisory services to PanEuropean within the audit scope during the engagement and for two years before it; the lead engagement partner rotates at least every five years (illustrative terms)
* Group Internal Audit assesses the Group DPO's independence, resources and absence of conflicts on substance; the Group DPO never assesses itself
* The Chief Privacy Officer and the process owners do not approve audit scope, select auditors or edit conclusions

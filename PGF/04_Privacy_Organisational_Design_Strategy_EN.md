# PGF-04 — Privacy Organisational Design Strategy

*Privacy Governance Framework (PGF) · Level 1 — Strategic Documents · Release 1.0.0-rc.2 · Regulatory reference date: 26 October 2026*

> **Status and use.** Part A is a reusable template. Part B is a worked example for a fictional organisation (PanEuropean Bank AG); the named implementation profile identifies a real product; the Bank, deployment, figures, dates and records in Part B are illustrative. Nothing in this document is legal advice or a statement about any real organisation, product or provider.

# **About This Document**

The Privacy Organisational Design Strategy is Document 4 of the Privacy Governance Framework (PGF). It establishes the case for a dedicated privacy organisational function and its place within the corporate structure — a strategic-level management and budgetary decision, without operational detail.

This document details the three-tier governance model (Oversight / Management / Execution) that the Privacy Governance Charter (Document 2, Section 9) introduces at summary level, and positions the two roles that sit outside the tiers: the independent Data Protection Officer (DPO) and the Processing Owner. It is operationalised through the Operational Role & Staffing Model (Document 26, Level 3), draws on the Privacy Role Competency Framework (Document 6) to define role types, and is aligned with the Privacy Governance Interface & Integration Model (Document 3) on reporting lines and committee affiliation.

# **How to Use This Document**

Part A is a drafting template: replace the bracketed placeholders and record the organisation's choices with their rationale. Part B shows the template completed for a fictional bank. Organisational choices that are policy preferences (for example, insourcing the DPO) are labelled as such and must not be presented as legal requirements.

| GUIDANCE  *Before developing this document, confirm that the Charter (Document 2) has been adopted at least in draft form, and that Section 3.11 of the Interface & Integration Model (Document 3) has recorded the Governing Body committees that oversee the programme and confirmed that the Privacy Risk Committee is a management-level committee — without this, the organisational positioning described below will have nothing to align against.* |

## **1. Purpose of This Document**

This document establishes the case for a dedicated privacy organisational function at ***[organisation name]*** and defines its place within the corporate structure. This is a strategic-level management and budgetary decision — the document does not describe operational procedures (these are fixed in the process map, Document 24) and does not assign specific individuals to roles (this is done by the Operational Role & Staffing Model, Document 26). This document answers three questions: what organisational structure is needed, how much it costs, and in what order it is rolled out.

| GUIDANCE  *If the organisation already has a compliance or risk management function that privacy naturally gravitates toward, explicitly state why establishing a separate function is warranted in this case — this is a standard question Executive Management and the Governing Body ask when the budget is adopted. The EPPO role can often be assigned to an existing executive; no separate new post is required.* |

## **2. Organisational Positioning**

| GUIDANCE  *Positioning should reduce the risk of DPO conflicts of interest by design, not rely solely on the good faith of a particular individual. The most common source of such risk is having the DPO report to a function whose own decisions the DPO must independently assess (for example, the Chief Data Officer, the CISO, or a business-line head). The specific administrative reporting line is detailed in Section 7, together with the reporting-line model for the other roles of the privacy function.* |
| :---- |

***Programme Ownership (EPPO)***

* ***[The privacy programme is run by the Executive Privacy Programme Owner (EPPO) — [title of the designated executive], who reports to [the responsible member of Executive Management]. The EPPO may be a Chief Privacy Officer, Head of Privacy or another designated executive; no separate new post is required. The EPPO is not the DPO]***

* ***[The EPPO is accountable for policies, processes, metrics, training and reporting (Charter, Section 8); the Privacy Operations Lead reports to the EPPO]***

***DPO Independence***

* ***[When choosing the DPO's administrative reporting line (Section 7), the organisation takes into account the risk of conflict of interests if the DPO reports to a function that determines the purposes and means of data processing — in particular, the Chief Data/Analytics Officer, the CISO or a business-line head (WP243 rev.01, §3.5; CJEU C-453/21 X-FAB)]***

* ***[The DPO reports directly to the highest management level — [Executive Management] (GDPR Art. 38(3)) — and, in addition, has an unrestricted right of access to the chair of the Governing Body's [Risk / Audit] Committee; see the Charter, Section 8]***

* ***[The DPO is designated by [Executive Management] on the basis of professional qualities and expert knowledge (Art. 37(5)); removal is decided by [Executive Management] [with the consent of the Governing Body, where its rules reserve such consent]. The DPO is not dismissed or penalised for performing its tasks (Art. 38(3)); any stricter national dismissal protection applies]***

* ***[The DPO receives the resources necessary to carry out its tasks, access to personal data and processing operations, and the resources to maintain its expert knowledge (Art. 38(2)), including a budget line under Section 8]***

* ***[Deputy arrangements — a deputy DPO with the same independence guarantees is designated to cover absences; the deputy exercises all DPO tasks during the absence, and the DPO contact point published under Art. 37(7) remains reachable at all times. Breach notification decisions are controller decisions (Charter, Section 8) and never wait for the DPO's return]***

***Governing Body Oversight***

The Governing Body oversees the programme through ***[its Risk Committee / Audit Committee]***, whose role is recorded in the Interface & Integration Model (Document 3, Section 3.11). The management-level Privacy Risk Committee (chaired by ***[the EPPO / a member of Executive Management]***) is not a Governing Body committee; the DPO attends it as an independent, non-voting adviser. For HR administration purposes (employment records, payroll) the DPO position may be formally housed within ***[the office of the Chief Executive / another unit that does not determine purposes and means of processing]***, which affects neither line reporting, nor functional independence, nor the DPO's direct access to the highest management level.

| GUIDANCE  *Distinguish three separate concepts that are often conflated: (1) line management — to whom the DPO reports as an employee, the choice of which is detailed in Section 7; (2) HR administration — in which department's structure the position is formally recorded for personnel-record purposes; (3) direct reporting and escalation — Executive Management as the highest management level, with access to the Governing Body committee. All three may differ from one another, but none may give the line manager influence over the content of the DPO's advice.* |
| :---- |

***Positioning of the Processing Owner***

* ***[The Processing Owner mandate does not create a new administrative reporting line — the business-line head remains within their own reporting line; the reporting-line model for this and other roles is detailed in Section 7]***

* ***[The Processing Owner role is deliberately outside the three-tier privacy governance model (Oversight/Management/Execution) introduced in Section 4 — see the Charter, Section 9]***

* ***[The Processing Owner's conflict of interest (taking processing decisions for initiatives of their own business line) is managed through the legal compliance gate (G-L), the DPO's mandatory advice on every DPIA, the EPPO's concurrence at the Medium tier and the escalation of High residual risk to Executive Management — see the Charter, Section 8, and Document 5, Section 7]***

* ***[The Processing Owner mandate is attached to the business-line head position, not to a specific individual: upon a planned or unplanned change of the head, the mandate transfers to the new head under the existing designation by [Executive Management], provided the new head meets the role profile (Document 6, Section 4.6); the transfer is recorded in Document 25, Section 10, and the Privacy Risk Committee is notified at its next meeting. If the business line is discontinued, merged, or restructured such that the position itself ceases to exist, [Executive Management] designates the new mandate holder on the EPPO's proposal (Document 5, row G9)]***

## **3. Insourcing/Outsourcing Principle**

| GUIDANCE  *This section establishes which privacy management functions the organisation performs in-house and which may be delegated to external providers. Without an explicit principle, outsourcing decisions are made on an ad hoc basis (typically driven by cost) and risk delegating a decision that belongs to the controller. Distinguish clearly between what the law requires and what is the organisation's policy choice. All subsequent sections of this document (headcount, budget, roadmap) are considered in light of the boundary established here.* |
| :---- |

***Boundary Criteria***

* ***[Criterion 1 — Controller decisions stay with the controller: decisions that the Charter (Section 8) assigns to Executive Management, the EPPO or a Processing Owner cannot be performed by an external provider, regardless of cost or provider qualifications; a provider may prepare them]***

* ***[Criterion 2 — Access to personal data combined with discretionary judgement: functions that require both access to personal data and substantive case-by-case judgement (rather than the application of a fixed methodology) are retained in-house unless a documented assessment supports outsourcing]***

* ***[Criterion 3 — Existence of a repeatable, documented methodology: functions performed according to a clearly described methodology, without the need to make a substantive decision each time, are candidates for outsourcing]***

* ***[Criterion 4 — Scarcity of rare expertise during the function's formative stage: narrowly specialised technical competencies not yet available in-house at an early stage (Section 9, Phase 0–1) may be covered by an external resource, with an internalisation plan where the organisation's policy prefers insourcing]***

***Decisions That Are Not Outsourced (controller decisions)***

* ***[The decision whether to notify the competent SA and data subjects of a personal data breach — a controller decision taken by the designated member of Executive Management on the DPO's advice (Data Breach Response Policy, Document 14)]***

* ***[The decision to stop, redesign or launch a processing activity, and acceptance of residual risk under the delegated tiers (Document 5, Section 7)]***

* ***[Ownership of the DPIA and of the evidence pack supporting a risk decision]***

* ***[Determining and assigning Accountable responsibility in the RACI matrix]***

* ***[Representing the organisation as controller before the supervisory authority — the DPO remains the SA's contact point (Art. 39(1)(e))]***

***The DPO Role — Legal Position and Policy Choice***

* ***[GDPR Art. 37(6) allows the DPO to be a staff member or to fulfil the tasks on the basis of a service contract. Whether to insource the DPO is therefore a policy choice of [organisation name], not a legal limit. This organisation's policy: [insourced DPO / external DPO under a service contract / insourced with external support], for the reasons recorded here: [rationale — e.g., scale, language coverage, continuity, cost]]***

* ***[Where an external DPO is engaged, the service contract preserves all DPO guarantees: independence, no instructions, no penalty for performing tasks, sufficient resources and access, confidentiality, an identified individual and a deputy, and easy accessibility from each establishment (Arts. 37(2), 38). Avoid contract terms that would let the provider be dismissed for the content of its advice]***

***Functions Eligible for Outsourcing***

* ***[DPO tasks under a service contract (Art. 37(6)), where chosen by policy — see above]***

* ***[Technical implementation of privacy-enhancing techniques and support of the automation tooling — provided that adoption of the methodology and acceptance of results remain in-house]***

* ***[Development and delivery of training programme content — provided that the EPPO approves the content after DPO advice]***

* ***[Specialist support to the independent audit (Document 27) — Independent Internal Audit remains accountable for the assurance opinion and may engage an external auditor for specialist topics]***

* ***[Initial handling (intake, logging, initial identity verification) of data subject requests — provided that the final substantive decision on the request remains in-house]***

* ***[External legal opinions on complex questions of regulatory interpretation]***

| GUIDANCE  *Any external provider engaged under this section itself becomes subject to the Vendor / Third-Party Privacy Risk Policy (Document 17) — including cases where the provider processes personal data in the course of delivering DPO services or technical support. Financial entities also classify the arrangement under their ICT third-party risk and outsourcing frameworks (for example, DORA Arts. 28–30) and record it in the corresponding register, without duplicating the privacy vendor record. Outsourcing a privacy function does not remove it from the scope of the privacy governance model — it adds the provider as a subject of risk assessment.* |

| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Tier** | **Composition** | **Cadence** | **Authority** |
| Oversight | ***[Governing Body and its Risk / Audit Committee]*** | ***[Quarterly / upon escalation]*** | ***[Oversees the programme; receives the EPPO's programme reports, the DPO's reports where the DPO considers it necessary, and Independent Internal Audit's assurance opinion; holds reserved consent matters only where its rules so provide; does not take operational decisions]*** |

## **7. Role Reporting-Line Model**

***Choice of the DPO's Administrative Reporting Line***

| GUIDANCE  *The organisation must explicitly choose to whom the DPO administratively reports, rather than leaving the question undetermined. Below is an analysis of typical options; the choice must be explicitly recorded and justified, not inherited by default from the historical structure. "Not recommended" describes an elevated risk of conflict of interests, not a formal prohibition: the conflict is assessed case by case on the DPO's actual tasks (CJEU C-453/21 X-FAB). If an organisation chooses such an option, it records the conflict assessment and compensating measures (see the variant box below). Whatever the administrative line, the DPO reports directly to the highest management level (Art. 38(3)).* |  |  |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Option** | **Assessment** | **Recommendation** |
| Executive Management / CEO (direct) | ***[Meets Art. 38(3) directly; eliminates sectoral conflict of interests; gives DPO advice maximum weight vis-à-vis operational management]*** | ***[Recommended default option]*** |
| COO | ***[Not the highest management level by itself; in some organisations the COO determines purposes and means of operational processing (WP243 rev.01, §3.5 lists COO among roles that may conflict when held by the DPO)]*** | ***[Acceptable only with documented direct access to Executive Management and a recorded conflict assessment]*** |
| CRO (Chief Risk Officer) | ***[The CRO usually does not determine purposes and means of processing, but may own credit-risk or other models that the DPO must assess; risk of competition between the overall risk appetite and the privacy risk tiers]*** | ***[Acceptable alternative after a recorded conflict assessment]*** |
| CDO (Chief Data Officer) | ***[The CDO typically determines the purposes and means of analytics processing and may own the platform whose outputs the DPO advises on — a self-review risk]*** | ***[Not recommended]*** |
| CISO | ***[The CISO makes decisions on technical security measures that are themselves subject to privacy assessment, and owns security-event handling that feeds breach decisions]*** | ***[Not recommended]*** |
| Business-line head | ***[The business line's products are themselves the primary subject of the DPO's advice]*** | ***[Not recommended]*** |

This organisation selects administrative reporting of the DPO to ***[Executive Management / CEO / other — state the selected option, the conflict assessment and the rationale]***.

> **Guidance — variant box: DPO administratively under a function that determines purposes and means.** Where an organisation, for documented reasons, keeps the DPO administratively under such a function (for example, the CDO), it records at least: (1) the case-by-case conflict assessment and its annual review; (2) direct reporting to Executive Management and access to the Governing Body committee that the line manager cannot restrict; (3) a performance review conducted by Executive Management and limited to conduct, availability and resource use — never the content of advice; (4) no influence of the line manager over DPIA advice or monitoring results; (5) an Independent Internal Audit check of actual influence. This variant carries a higher supervisory challenge risk than the recommended default.

## **8. Budget Model**

| GUIDANCE  *The budget is broken down into line items rather than given as a lump sum — this lets Executive Management and the Governing Body see what the requested amount consists of and review line items individually. The DPO's resources are a legal requirement (Art. 38(2)), not a discretionary item.* |  |  |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Budget Line Item (operational expenditure)** | **Composition** | **Cadence** |
| ***[Privacy function payroll]*** | ***[Salaries of the EPPO (or the privacy share of an existing executive), DPO and deputy, Privacy Operations Lead, Privacy Officer(s), Privacy Engineer(s), Data Protection Coordinators — the insourced roles under Section 3]*** | ***[Annually, with review]*** |

## **1. Purpose of This Document**

This document establishes the case for a dedicated privacy management organisational function at PanEuropean Bank AG and defines its place within the bank's corporate structure. Previously, privacy at PanEuropean was distributed across Legal, IT, and local branch compliance functions with no unified structure, and programme-management tasks had accumulated with the Group DPO. This document establishes a dedicated privacy function led by the Chief Privacy Officer as EPPO, positions the Group DPO as an independent statutory function, and sizes the structure to the bank's scale (5 countries of presence, retail and anti-fraud systems supported by the Bank's privacy engineering platform, PEP, illustrative).

## **2. Organisational Positioning**

***Programme Ownership (EPPO)***

* The EPPO mandate is held by the Chief Privacy Officer (CPO), who reports to the CRO as the Management Board member responsible for the privacy programme. The CPO position was created in October 2026 by converting a budgeted Phase 3 position (Section 5); there is no net increase in headcount

* The Privacy Operations Lead, the Privacy Officer(s) and the Privacy Engineer(s) report to the CPO line (Section 7); the Group DPO does not manage the programme team

***DPO Independence***

* The Group DPO reports directly to the Management Board — administratively through its Chair (the CEO) — which is the highest management level of PanEuropean Bank AG within the meaning of Art. 38(3); in addition, the Group DPO has an unrestricted right of access to the chairs of the Risk Committee and the Audit Committee of the Supervisory Board

* The Group DPO does not report to the CDO, the CISO, the CRO or any business-line head; the conflict assessment under Art. 38(6) was recorded on designation and is reviewed annually

* The Group DPO is designated by the Management Board; removal is decided by the Management Board and, under the Supervisory Board's rules of procedure, requires the Supervisory Board's consent (AktG §111(4)). The Group DPO is not dismissed or penalised for performing its tasks (Art. 38(3)); as a mandatory DPO in Germany, the special dismissal protection of §38(2) with §6(4) BDSG applies

* A Deputy Group DPO, located in the Group DPO's office and enjoying the same independence guarantees, exercises all DPO tasks during the Group DPO's absence (illness, leave, vacancy); the published DPO contact point remains reachable at all times. Breach notification decisions are taken by the CRO (deputy: COO) on the advice of the Group DPO or the Deputy Group DPO and never wait for the Group DPO's return; an absence exceeding 60 days is reported to the Management Board and the Risk Committee of the Supervisory Board

***Governing Body Oversight***

The Supervisory Board oversees the programme through its Risk Committee and its Audit Committee, as recorded in the Interface & Integration Model (Section 3.11). The Privacy Risk Committee is a management-level committee chaired by the CRO; the Group DPO attends it as an independent, non-voting adviser. For HR administration purposes (employment records, payroll) the Group DPO position is housed in the office of the Chief Executive, which affects neither line reporting, nor functional independence, nor the Group DPO's direct access to the Management Board and the Supervisory Board committees.

***Positioning of the Processing Owner***

* The Processing Owner mandate does not create a new administrative reporting line — the heads of Credit Decisions, Digital Banking, Marketing, Customer Service, Fraud Prevention, Insurance Distribution, Compliance (AML screening), Payment Services, Human Resources and Branch Network, the CIO for IT-operated processing, and the CPO for the privacy function's own processing (holders — Document 25, Section 10), remain within their own reporting lines; the reporting-line model for this and other PanEuropean roles is detailed in Section 7

* The Processing Owner role is deliberately outside PanEuropean's three-tier privacy governance model (Oversight/Management/Execution) introduced in Section 4 — see the Charter, Section 9

* The Processing Owner's conflict of interest (taking processing decisions for initiatives of their own business line) is managed through G-L, the Group DPO's mandatory advice on every DPIA, the CPO's concurrence at the Medium tier and the escalation of High residual risk to the CRO — see the Charter, Section 8

* The Processing Owner mandate is attached to the head-of-business-line position, not to a specific individual: upon a change of head, the mandate transfers to the new head under the Management Board's existing designation, provided the new head meets the Processing Owner role profile (Document 6, Section 4.6); the transfer is recorded in Document 25, Section 10, and the Privacy Risk Committee is notified at its next meeting. If a business line is discontinued or merged (for example, Digital Banking merging with another line), the Management Board designates the new mandate holder on the CPO's proposal (RACI row G9)

## **3. Insourcing/Outsourcing Principle**

***Decisions PanEuropean Does Not Outsource (controller decisions)***

* The decision whether to notify the HBDI (lead SA) and customers of a personal data breach — taken by the CRO (deputy: COO) on the Group DPO's advice, see the Data Breach Response Policy, Document 14; DORA major-incident reporting to the financial supervisor is a separate track owned by the ICT Risk Control function

* The decision to stop, redesign or launch a processing activity, and acceptance of residual risk under the delegated tiers (RACI Matrix, Section 7)

* Ownership of DPIAs for scoring and anti-fraud models and of the evidence packs supporting risk decisions

* Determining Accountable responsibility in PanEuropean's RACI matrix

* Representing PanEuropean as controller before the HBDI and the concerned SAs — the Group DPO remains their contact point

***The Group DPO Role — Legal Position and Policy Choice***

* Art. 37(6) GDPR would allow PanEuropean to engage an external DPO under a service contract. PanEuropean's policy is to insource the Group DPO, for reasons of scale (five countries, high DPIA volume), continuity of the deputy arrangement and language coverage. This is a policy choice, not a legal limit

* From 1 March 2024 to 15 June 2024 (Phase 0), PanEuropean used an external DPO under a service contract that preserved all Art. 38 guarantees; the insourced Group DPO was designated with effect from 16 June 2024, so there was no period without a designated DPO

***Functions PanEuropean Outsources***

* Technical support of PEP and implementation of PET techniques — provided by an external technical support provider (illustrative) under a technical support contract classified under the bank's ICT third-party risk and outsourcing framework (DORA Arts. 28–30); adoption of the risk assessment methodology and acceptance of results remain in-house (CPO and Privacy Risk Committee)

* Development of content for the annual privacy training programme — an external e-learning provider; the content is approved by the CPO after the Group DPO's advice

* Specialist support to Group Internal Audit for PET-related audit topics — an external audit firm engaged by Group Internal Audit, which remains accountable for the assurance opinion

* Initial intake and identity verification of DSR requesters through the contact centre — the final substantive decision on the request is made by the Privacy Officer of the relevant country

* External legal opinions on cross-border data transfers to India — an engaged law firm

Each of the external providers listed above is itself subject to PanEuropean's Vendor / Third-Party Privacy Risk Policy — outsourcing a privacy function does not remove it from the scope of the governance model; it adds the provider as a subject of risk assessment.

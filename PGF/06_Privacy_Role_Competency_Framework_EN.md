# PGF-06 — Privacy Role Competency Framework

*Privacy Governance Framework (PGF) · Level 1 — Strategic Documents · Version 1.0.0 · Regulatory reference date: 26 October 2026*

> **Status and use.** Part A is a reusable template. Part B is a worked example for a fictional organisation (PanEuropean Bank AG); the named implementation profile identifies a real product; the Bank, deployment, figures, dates and records in Part B are illustrative. Nothing in this document is legal advice or a statement about any real organisation, product or provider.

# **About This Document**

The Privacy Role Competency Framework is Document 6 of the Privacy Governance Framework (PGF). It establishes the qualification requirements for every privacy role set out in the RACI Matrix (Document 5), turning the distribution of responsibility into a verifiable requirement placed on people, rather than an abstract job title.

This document relies on the roles defined in the Charter (Document 2, Sections 8 and 9) and allocated in the RACI Matrix (Document 5), feeds the Privacy Awareness & Training Programme (Document 30), and is operationalised in the Operational Role & Staffing Model (Document 26), where the competency requirements are mapped against specific incumbent positions and their assessment results.

# **How to Use This Document**

Part A is a drafting template: replace the bracketed placeholders. Part B shows the template completed for a fictional bank. Qualification requirements are vendor-neutral: where a named certification is mentioned, it is an example of a recognised certification, never the only acceptable route and never a legal requirement.

| GUIDANCE  *Before developing this document, ensure that the RACI Matrix (Document 5) has been adopted at least in draft form — without a list of roles and an R/A/C/I distribution, there is nothing for competency profiles to be mapped against. Every qualification requirement in this document must be explained by a specific line of the RACI Matrix or by a legal requirement (for example, GDPR Art. 37(5) for the DPO), not by general reasoning about desirable qualifications.* |

## **1. Purpose of This Document**

This document establishes the qualification requirements for every privacy management role at ***[organisation name]*** set out in the RACI Matrix (Document 5). It answers the question of what qualifications a person must hold to lawfully and diligently perform a role, rather than merely bear its title. This document does not assign specific individuals to roles (that is done by the Operational Role & Staffing Model, Document 26) and does not describe the content of the training programme (that is set out in the Privacy Awareness & Training Programme, Document 30) — it establishes the requirements that the training programme must meet and against which a role candidate is assessed.

| GUIDANCE  *The R/A/C/I distribution in the RACI Matrix is meaningful only when the role marked Accountable is actually capable of bearing that accountability — that is, holds sufficient qualification to substantively assess the decision, not merely the formal right to sign off. This document makes that condition verifiable. For the DPO, the legal standard is "professional qualities and, in particular, expert knowledge of data protection law and practices" proportionate to the processing (Art. 37(5); WP243 rev.01, §2.5); no specific certification is legally required.* |
| :---- |

## **2. Principles for Building Competency Profiles**

| GUIDANCE  *Without explicit principles, competency requirements quickly degenerate into a list of desirable but unverifiable traits ("strong communication skills," "strategic thinking") — wording of this kind cannot be used in candidate selection or in auditing role compliance.* |
| :---- |

* ***[The verifiability principle — every requirement must be capable of confirmation by a document, examination, or objective criterion (degree, certificate, length of service, assessed work sample, test result), not by the candidate's self-assessment]***

* ***[The vendor-neutrality principle — mandatory qualifications are expressed as "a recognised privacy certification or equivalent demonstrable expertise"; named certifications (for example, IAPP credentials or national DPO certification schemes) are examples only; no certificate issued by a tool vendor is a condition for holding a role. Proficiency in the organisation's own tools is confirmed by an internal assessment]***

* ***[The RACI-traceability principle — every requirement in a role profile must be explained by a specific line of the RACI Matrix (Document 5) or by law: if a role is Accountable for a decision requiring legal assessment, the profile must require competence covering that assessment]***

* ***[The mandatory/desirable-separation principle — the profile explicitly separates requirements without which a candidate cannot be appointed to the role (mandatory) from requirements that improve the quality of performance but do not block appointment (desirable)]***

* ***[The authority-proportionate-to-qualification principle — the level of authority to decide (Section 4) may not exceed the qualification confirmed for the role; an expansion of authority without a corresponding increase in qualification is not permitted. This principle does not give the DPO decision authority: the DPO's role is advisory by law]***

* ***[The periodic-reconfirmation principle — qualification is confirmed not once, at hiring, but on a recurring basis (Section 6); a lapse triggers a funded remediation plan; for the DPO, a lapse never leads to suspension or penalty (Art. 38(2)–(3))]***

## **4. Competency Profiles by Role**

***4.1 DPO***

Is Responsible and Accountable for the quality and timeliness of its own statutory tasks (Art. 39): informing and advising; monitoring compliance, including the compliance audits it organises (Document 5, row G6.1); advice on every DPIA (Document 5, Section 5.2, C\*); cooperation with and acting as the contact point for the supervisory authority; acting as the contact point for data subjects (Document 5, Section 5.3); its own reports to the highest management level (row G8.2); its own awareness-raising (row S8.1). Level of decision authority: ***[none on the controller's behalf — the DPO does not authorise processing, accept risk, determine purposes or means, notify breaches as decision-maker, or hold a veto; it advises with a weight that the decision-maker must consider, and the decision-maker records reasons when departing from the advice (Charter, Section 8; Document 5, Section 7)]***. The DPO's effectiveness is assessed on the timeliness, coverage and documentation of its statutory tasks (Document 28, Section 6.6); the content of its advice, objections later shown to be unfounded, and escalations are never used as performance or disciplinary criteria. ***[The deputy DPO arrangement is governed by the Organisational Design Strategy (Document 4, Section 2); the requirements of this profile apply in full to the deputy DPO.]***

| Requirement category | Requirement |
| :---- | :---- |
| ***[Formal education]*** | ***[A university degree in law, information technology, or a related field; the presence or absence of a law degree specifically is not a formal barrier where equivalent expertise exists]*** |
| ***[Mandatory — expert knowledge (Art. 37(5))]*** | ***[Expert knowledge of data protection law and practices proportionate to the organisation's processing, demonstrated by a recognised privacy certification or equivalent demonstrable expertise (for example, a record of DPO or data protection counsel work, publications, or an assessed case portfolio). Examples of recognised certifications: IAPP CIPP/E; national DPO certification schemes; ISO/IEC 17024-accredited DPO personnel certification schemes. None of these is legally required]*** |
| ***[Desirable certifications]*** | ***[A privacy programme-management certification (for example, IAPP CIPM) or equivalent; an AI-governance credential (for example, IAPP AIGP) or equivalent where AI systems are material]*** |
| ***[Experience]*** | ***[At least [X] years of experience in data protection, compliance, or a related regulatory function, including experience interacting with supervisory authorities]*** |
| ***[Sector and national-law knowledge]*** | ***[Knowledge of the sector's rules relevant to data protection (e.g., banking secrecy, AML/KYC record-keeping) and of the national data protection law of each jurisdiction covered, or access to qualified support for jurisdictions outside the DPO's own expertise]*** |
| ***[Language and accessibility]*** | ***[Ability to communicate with supervisory authorities and data subjects in the languages of the jurisdictions covered, directly or with supported channels (WP243 rev.01, §2.3)]*** |
| ***[Specialised competencies]*** | ***[An understanding of the risk methodology (Charter, Sections 2 and 7; Document 18) and of the mandatory gates sufficient for a substantive, not merely formal, review of DPIAs; AI literacy sufficient to advise on AI systems that process personal data (see Section 4.8)]*** |
| ***[Independence]*** | ***[See Section 5 — the independence requirements apply to the DPO first and foremost]*** |

***4.2 Privacy Operations Lead***

Is Accountable for day-to-day privacy operations and owns Risk Calculation Projects (Document 18) — for example, intake and triage of requests, the standard track of a risk calculation project, calculation of residual risk, PET application, data subject requests, the RoPA and the evidence pack (Document 5, Sections 5 and 6). Reports to the EPPO. Level of decision authority: ***[operational decisions assigned line by line in Document 5; no authority to accept residual risk on the rights-and-freedoms track, which belongs to the Processing Owner or Executive Management (Document 5, Section 7)]***.

| Requirement category | Requirement |
| :---- | :---- |
| ***[Formal education]*** | ***[A university degree; a specialisation in data protection, risk management, or process management]*** |
| ***[Mandatory]*** | ***[A recognised privacy programme-management certification or equivalent demonstrable expertise (example: IAPP CIPM)]*** |
| ***[Desirable]*** | ***[A recognised privacy-law certification or equivalent (example: IAPP CIPP/E)]*** |
| ***[Experience]*** | ***[At least [X] years of experience in the operational management of compliance, risk, or quality processes]*** |
| ***[Specialised competencies]*** | ***[The ability to determine whether a risk calculation project or processing request falls on the standard or non-standard track (Document 5, Section 5.1) and to route it correctly to the EPPO and the Privacy Risk Committee; working knowledge of the mandatory gates; AI literacy (Section 4.8)]*** |
| ***[Independence]*** | ***[See Section 5 — limited form: a prohibition on holding, at the same time, a role whose decisions are assessed within a given process]*** |

***4.3 Privacy Officer***

Is Responsible for performing DPIAs with the business, processing data subject requests, maintaining the RoPA and vendor privacy due diligence (Document 5, Sections 5.2, 5.3 and 6). When performing monitoring tasks for the DPO (Document 5, row G6.1), acts under the DPO's direction. Level of decision authority: ***[no right to decide on residual risk; prepares analyses for the Privacy Operations Lead, the Processing Owner and Legal]***.

| Requirement category | Requirement |
| :---- | :---- |
| ***[Formal education]*** | ***[A university degree; in law, compliance, or a related field]*** |
| ***[Mandatory]*** | ***[A recognised privacy-law certification or equivalent demonstrable expertise within [X] months of appointment (example: IAPP CIPP/E or a national certification)]*** |
| ***[Experience]*** | ***[[X] year(s) of experience in data protection or compliance, or a completed internal onboarding programme in the absence of experience]*** |
| ***[National-law competence (country officers)]*** | ***[Working knowledge of the national data protection law, sector rules and supervisory-authority practice of the officer's country, and working proficiency in its official language]*** |
| ***[Specialised competencies]*** | ***[Working proficiency in the DPIA methodology used by the organisation; an understanding of the lawful bases and conditions relevant to the product line; AI literacy (Section 4.8)]*** |
| ***[Independence]*** | ***[See Section 5 — limited form: a prohibition on holding, at the same time, a role whose decisions are assessed within a given DPIA]*** |

***4.4 Privacy Engineer***

Is Responsible for selecting and applying privacy-enhancing techniques (PETs), configuring new techniques, preparing risk calculation projects, and operating the automation tooling (Document 5, Sections 5.1, 5.2 and row S9). Level of decision authority: ***[no right to decide on residual risk; performs the technical implementation and reports technical results, including failures of mandatory metrics or known feasible attacks, to the Privacy Operations Lead]***.

| Requirement category | Requirement |
| :---- | :---- |
| ***[Formal education]*** | ***[A university degree in a technical field — computer science, data engineering, statistics, or a related area]*** |
| ***[Mandatory]*** | ***[A recognised privacy-engineering certification or equivalent demonstrable expertise (examples: IAPP CIPT, ISACA CDPSE, or an assessed portfolio of privacy-engineering work)]*** |
| ***[Tool proficiency]*** | ***[Internal assessment of proficiency in the organisation's automation tooling and its documented limitations, before independent authorisation to configure PET techniques; no vendor-issued certificate is required]*** |
| ***[Experience]*** | ***[[X] year(s) of hands-on experience in data engineering, including work with pseudonymisation, anonymisation and synthetic-data techniques]*** |
| ***[Specialised competencies]*** | ***[Statistical disclosure control, differential privacy (privacy unit, adjacency, mechanism and accountant, composition — NIST SP 800-226), re-identification and membership-inference testing, and the limits of these tests; the distinction between pseudonymised data, anonymous information and synthetic data (Document 18); AI literacy (Section 4.8)]*** |
| ***[Independence]*** | ***[See Section 5 — limited form: a prohibition on holding, at the same time, a role responsible for designing or developing the data-processing system whose residual risk the Privacy Engineer subsequently assesses]*** |

***4.5 Data Protection Coordinator***

A distributed role within a business unit with a dual reporting line (the "DPC"; see Document 4, Section 7). Level of decision authority: ***[none; the role serves as a communication channel between the business unit and the privacy function, not a decision point]***. Unlike the Processing Owner (Section 4.6) — also a role within the business unit, but assigned to its head and vested with delegated decision authority — the DPC never decides on residual risk.

| Requirement category | Requirement |
| :---- | :---- |
| ***[Formal education]*** | ***[Not set separately from the requirements of the underlying position within the business unit]*** |
| ***[Mandatory]*** | ***[Internal certification under the training programme (Document 30); external certification is not mandatory]*** |
| ***[Experience]*** | ***[Not set separately; the role is held concurrently with the incumbent's primary position in the business unit]*** |
| ***[Specialised competencies]*** | ***[The ability to recognise a situation requiring escalation to the privacy function (including a possible personal data breach or a change that triggers DPIA triage), and not to decide independently on the permissibility of processing; where the role exists at several organisational instances, the requirement may be broken out into a separate table by instance]*** |
| ***[Independence]*** | ***[See Section 5 — the dual reporting line built into the role itself is offset by the role's lack of decision authority, not by a separate independence requirement]*** |

***4.6 Processing Owner***

Is Accountable for processing-level decisions of its business line within the delegation granted by the controller (Charter, Section 8): launching or changing processing, owning the DPIA (Document 5, Section 5.2), human review of automated decisions where the line takes them (Section 5.3), consent design and withdrawal for its processing (Section 5.5), retention execution and engagement of processors (Section 6), and acceptance of residual risk at the Low tier and, with EPPO concurrence, at the Medium tier (Document 5, Section 7). A mandate designated by ***[Executive Management]***, not a separate staff position; assigned to the incumbent head of the business line or their qualified, authorised deputy. Level of decision authority: ***[Low — independently; Medium — with EPPO concurrence; High — proposes only, the decision is taken by one named member of Executive Management (or the Privacy Risk Committee where a written delegation designates it) after G-36 has been evaluated and, where the Art. 36 test is met, after prior consultation; Critical — not acceptable (stop or redesign). No decision may be taken before G-L has been passed. Where the decision departs from the DPO's advice, the Processing Owner records the reasons (Document 5, Section 7)]***

| Requirement category | Requirement |
| :---- | :---- |
| ***[Formal education]*** | ***[Not set separately from the requirements of the business-line head position itself]*** |
| ***[Mandatory]*** | ***[Completion, on designation, of a role briefing covering the Charter (Section 8), the mandatory gates (Charter, Section 11), the acceptance tiers (Document 5, Section 7) and this profile, confirmed by a short assessment]*** |
| ***[Experience]*** | ***[Not set separately; the role is held concurrently with the incumbent's primary position as head of the business line, or by their qualified, authorised deputy]*** |
| ***[Specialised competencies]*** | ***[The ability to weigh the expected benefit of lawful processing against the residual risk to individuals and to reach a reasoned decision within the delegated tier; an understanding that benefit never cures a G-L failure or replaces prior consultation; the ability to articulate written reasons when departing from the DPO's advice; AI literacy appropriate to the AI systems the line deploys (Section 4.8)]*** |
| ***[Independence]*** | ***[Not applicable by design — the compensating controls are G-L, the DPO's mandatory advice on every DPIA, recorded reasons for any departure, EPPO concurrence at Medium, escalation of High risk to Executive Management, and the DPO's right to escalate directly to Executive Management]*** |

***4.7 Executive Privacy Programme Owner (EPPO)***

Is Accountable for operating the privacy programme (Document 5, Section 4 and most of Section 6): strategy, risk appetite proposals, methodology, policies, metrics, training, the Privacy Risk Register, breach qualification and recording, and programme reporting; gives concurrence to Medium-tier acceptance and prepares prior consultations (Document 5, Sections 5.2 and 7). A designated executive — for example, a Chief Privacy Officer or Head of Privacy; no separate new post is required. Level of decision authority: ***[programme decisions assigned in Document 5; concurrence at the Medium tier; no authority to accept High residual risk unless the EPPO is itself the named member of Executive Management; never the DPO]***.

| Requirement category | Requirement |
| :---- | :---- |
| ***[Formal education]*** | ***[A university degree; a specialisation in law, risk management, information management or a related field]*** |
| ***[Mandatory]*** | ***[A recognised privacy programme-management certification or equivalent demonstrable expertise (example: IAPP CIPM), and working knowledge of the data protection law of the main jurisdiction]*** |
| ***[Experience]*** | ***[At least [X] years of management experience in compliance, risk or privacy, including leading a cross-functional programme and reporting to Executive Management and a Governing Body committee]*** |
| ***[Specialised competencies]*** | ***[The mandatory gates and their legal basis; the two-track risk model (Document 18); AI literacy and AI-governance interfaces (Section 4.8); for financial entities, the separation of GDPR breach notification from DORA ICT-incident reporting (Document 14)]*** |
| ***[Independence]*** | ***[See Section 5 — the EPPO may not be the DPO and may not be the Chief Audit Executive]*** |

***4.8 Cross-Role Requirement — AI Literacy***

***[Every role in this document that designs, deploys, assesses or advises on AI systems processing personal data holds AI literacy appropriate to the role: how the systems work, their limitations and failure modes, the data protection risks they create (including Art. 22), and the organisation's AI-governance interfaces (Document 3, Section 3.5). This framework requires it as a competence; it also supports the AI literacy measures referred to in Art. 4 of the AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689), which the organisation applies in the version in force, including as amended by the AI Omnibus. AI literacy is confirmed through the training programme (Document 30).]***

## **5. Independence Requirements**

| GUIDANCE  *The independence requirements apply first and foremost to the DPO, since this role must be protected from the influence of functions whose decisions the DPO advises on and monitors. The choice of the DPO's administrative reporting line is set out in the Organisational Design Strategy (Document 4, Section 7) — this section restates it as a requirement placed on the candidate and on the organisation.* |
| :---- |

* ***[A DPO candidate may not simultaneously hold a position that determines the purposes and means of data processing — the absence of such a dual role is verified at designation and reconfirmed annually. Under WP243 rev.01, §3.5, positions typically considered conflicting include chief executive, chief operating, chief financial, and chief medical officer, as well as heads of marketing, HR, or IT — the list is illustrative, not exhaustive: the same rule extends to any lower-level role that, in substance, determines the purposes and means of data processing. Conflicts are assessed case by case on the DPO's actual tasks (CJEU C-453/21 X-FAB; EDPB Coordinated Enforcement Action 2023 report)]***

* ***[A DPO candidate must not have family or financial ties to the heads of functions whose decisions the DPO advises on, where such ties could create a conflict of interests]***

* ***[A change in the DPO's administrative reporting line (Document 4, Section 7) does not remove the requirements of this section — independence is assessed on the merits, not by the formal reporting line]***

* ***[The DPO is not dismissed or penalised for performing its tasks (Art. 38(3)); WP243 rev.01, §3.4 explains that penalties may take many forms, such as absence or delay of promotion, or denial of benefits. Any disciplinary or personnel measure concerning the DPO is decided by [Executive Management], not by the administrative line manager alone, after the DPO has been heard; where the DPO considers that a measure relates to the performance of its tasks, the chair of [the Governing Body committee] is informed. The content of the DPO's advice, objections later shown to be unfounded, and escalations are excluded from any performance or disciplinary assessment. This safeguard does not bar measures taken on grounds unrelated to the performance of the DPO's tasks; any stricter national protection applies]***

* ***[The organisation funds the DPO's maintenance of expert knowledge, including certification upkeep and continuing professional education (Art. 38(2)); a lapse in a certificate or in CPE does not suspend the DPO or the deputy DPO from their tasks]***

* ***[The DPO is bound by secrecy or confidentiality concerning the performance of its tasks (Art. 38(5)); a DPO candidate must be able to handle confidential requests and decide, on a need-to-know basis, what is shared with the EPPO or management, using redaction or aggregate reporting where appropriate (Charter, Section 8)]***

* ***[The EPPO may not be the DPO, and neither may be the Chief Audit Executive]***

* ***[For the Privacy Operations Lead, Privacy Officer, and Privacy Engineer, the independence requirements apply in limited form — a prohibition on holding, at the same time, a role whose decisions or technical work they assess within a given process, without the general protection against instructions that applies to the DPO]***

* ***[By design, the Data Protection Coordinator role is administratively part of the very business unit whose decisions it helps assess (dual reporting line, Document 4, Section 7) — which is why the DPC is never vested with decision authority: the absence of authority (Section 4.5), not an independence requirement, is the compensating mechanism for this role]***

* ***[By design, the Processing Owner (Section 4.6) does not possess independence from the business line whose decisions it takes — a deliberate feature of the role: the compensating mechanisms are G-L, the DPO's mandatory advice, recorded reasons for departures, EPPO concurrence at Medium, escalation of High risk to Executive Management, and the DPO's right to escalate directly to Executive Management (Charter, Section 8)]***

## **6. Periodic Requalification Requirements**

| GUIDANCE  *Periodic requalification turns role compliance from a one-off event (hiring) into an ongoing process. A failure to reconfirm qualification within the set deadline triggers a time-bound, funded remediation plan. For roles other than the DPO, a specific delegated authorisation that depends on the lapsed qualification (for example, independent configuration of PET techniques) may be paused until the gap is remedied. For the DPO and deputy DPO there is no suspension: the controller must not be left without a functioning DPO, and a lapse is not a sanction for performing tasks (Art. 38(3)).* |  |  |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Role** | **Reconfirmation frequency** | **Reconfirmation requirement** |
| ***[DPO and deputy DPO]*** | ***[Annually]*** | ***[Confirmation of continuing professional education (CPE) and, where a certification is held, its renewal under the issuing body's cycle — funded by the organisation (Art. 38(2)); a lapse triggers support, not suspension]*** |
| ***[EPPO]*** | ***[Annually]*** | ***[Confirmation of CPE; briefing on regulatory changes affecting the programme]*** |
| ***[Privacy Operations Lead]*** | ***[Annually]*** | ***[Confirmation of CPE; renewal of any certification held under the issuing body's cycle]*** |
| ***[Privacy Officer]*** | ***[Annually]*** | ***[Confirmation of CPE; internal assessment of working proficiency in the DPIA methodology and national-law updates]*** |
| ***[Privacy Engineer]*** | ***[Annually]*** | ***[Confirmation of CPE; internal assessment of working proficiency in the current version of the automation tooling and its documented limitations]*** |
| ***[Data Protection Coordinator]*** | ***[Annually]*** | ***[Completion of the updated version of the training programme, Document 30]*** |
| ***[Processing Owner]*** | ***[On designation and after any material change to the Charter, the gates or the acceptance tiers]*** | ***[Role briefing and short assessment (Section 4.6)]*** |

The content of the programme covering the annual reconfirmation is set out in the Privacy Awareness & Training Programme (Document 30) — this document establishes only the frequency and the fact that reconfirmation is mandatory, not the content of the programme itself.

## **7. Relationship to Other Documents in the Model**

This document is built on the RACI Matrix (Document 5) and turns the distribution of responsibility into a verifiable qualification requirement. This document:

* ***[Relies on the roles set out in the RACI Matrix (Document 5, Section 3) — it does not introduce new roles and does not redefine the R/A/D/C/I distribution]***

* ***[Feeds the Privacy Awareness & Training Programme (Document 30) — the training programme is built to cover the competency requirements set out here, rather than an arbitrary set of topics]***

* ***[Is operationalised in the Operational Role & Staffing Model (Document 26) — there, the competency requirements are mapped against the assessment results of specific incumbent positions]***

* ***[The decision authority of the Processing Owner and the EPPO (Sections 4.6–4.7) follows the acceptance table in Section 7 of the RACI Matrix and the decision rows in its Section 5.2; this document does not redefine either; for other roles, authority follows the line-by-line allocation in Sections 4–6 of the RACI Matrix]***

* ***[Relies on the mandates established by the Charter (Section 8) and on the reporting-line analysis in the Organisational Design Strategy (Document 4, Section 7)]***

| GUIDANCE  *If the Operational Role & Staffing Model (Document 26) records an incumbent position that does not meet the requirements of this document, that is a sign of a staffing gap that must be closed (hiring, training, temporary external support under Document 4, Section 3) — not a reason to lower the requirements of this document.* |

## **1. Purpose of This Document**

This document establishes the qualification requirements for every privacy management role at PanEuropean Bank AG set out in the RACI Matrix. Before this document was adopted, hiring for the Privacy Officer role across PanEuropean's countries of presence followed different, informal criteria — this document establishes uniform requirements for all five countries, with country-specific national-law and language requirements.

## **2. Principles for Building Competency Profiles**

* The verifiability principle — the Group DPO's expert knowledge is confirmed by a recognised certification record (for PanEuropean's current Group DPO, an IAPP CIPP/E certificate number) or by an assessed record of equivalent expertise, not by the candidate's self-assessment

* The vendor-neutrality principle — no certificate issued by a tool vendor is a condition for any role; Privacy Engineers' proficiency in the Bank's privacy engineering platform (PEP, illustrative) is confirmed by an internal assessment run by the Privacy Operations Lead

* The RACI-traceability principle — the requirement for the Privacy Engineer to be proficient in de-identification and synthetic-data techniques is explained by the row "Selecting and Applying PET Techniques via PEP" (RACI Matrix, Section 5.2), where the Privacy Engineer is Responsible

* The mandatory/desirable-separation principle — a recognised privacy-law certification or equivalent is mandatory for the Privacy Officer within 6 months of appointment; a programme-management certification for the same role is desirable but does not block appointment

* The authority-proportionate-to-qualification principle — a Privacy Officer cannot obtain the right to decide on residual risk merely by obtaining a certification: by design (Section 4.3), the role lacks that right regardless of qualification, and expanding it would require changing the RACI model itself

* The periodic-reconfirmation principle — if a Privacy Engineer's annual tool-proficiency assessment lapses, the engineer's authorisation to configure new PET techniques on PEP is paused until reassessment; if the Group DPO's CPE confirmation lapses, PanEuropean funds the catch-up and the Group DPO continues to perform all tasks

## **4. Competency Profiles by Role**

***4.1 Group DPO***

Is Responsible and Accountable for the quality and timeliness of its own statutory tasks: advice to the Management Board, business lines and staff; monitoring compliance with the GDPR, the BDSG and national law in all five countries, including the compliance audits it organises (RACI row G6.1); advice on every DPIA, including `DPIA-CS-TRAIN-01` and `DPIA-CS-LAUNCH-01` for credit scoring (RACI Section 5.2); cooperation with and acting as the contact point for the HBDI as lead SA and the concerned SAs; acting as the contact point for customers (RACI Section 5.3); its own reports to the Management Board (row G8.2). Level of decision authority: none on the bank's behalf — the Group DPO does not approve DPIAs or evidence packs, block launches, approve transfers or decide on breach notification; those decisions sit with the Processing Owners, the CRO, General Counsel and the Management Board (RACI Sections 5–7), and any departure from the Group DPO's advice is recorded with reasons. The Group DPO's effectiveness is assessed on timeliness, coverage and documentation of its tasks (Privacy Metrics Dashboard, Document 28, Section 6.6); the content of its advice, objections later shown to be unfounded, and escalations are excluded. The requirements of this profile apply in full to the Deputy Group DPO (Organisational Design Strategy, Document 4, Section 2).

| Requirement category | Requirement |
| :---- | :---- |
| Formal education | A university degree in law, or a university degree in information technology together with demonstrated experience in the financial-sector regulatory field |
| Mandatory — expert knowledge (Art. 37(5)) | A recognised privacy-law certification or equivalent demonstrable expertise, covering EU data protection law and practice (example: IAPP CIPP/E; a German DPO certification scheme or another ISO/IEC 17024-accredited scheme is equally acceptable) |
| Desirable | A programme-management certification (example: IAPP CIPM) or equivalent; an AI-governance credential (example: IAPP AIGP) or equivalent, given credit scoring and the customer-service assistant |
| Experience | At least 7 years of experience in data protection or banking compliance, including at least 2 years of direct engagement with a data protection supervisory authority |
| Sector and national-law knowledge | BDSG and German banking-secrecy and AML record-keeping rules; working access to qualified national-law support for France, Italy, Spain and Poland; awareness of DORA ICT-incident reporting sufficient to keep it separate from GDPR breach notification |
| Language and accessibility | Professional German and English; customers and SAs in the other four countries are served through the Deputy Group DPO and local-language contact channels |
| Specialised competencies | An understanding of the risk methodology (PanEuropean Charter, Sections 2 and 7) sufficient for a substantive review of DPIAs for scoring models; AI literacy (Section 4.8), including Art. 22 safeguards for automated credit decisions |
| Independence | See Section 5 |

***4.2 Privacy Operations Lead***

Is Accountable for day-to-day privacy operations at PanEuropean and owns Risk Calculation Projects on PEP (RACI Matrix, Sections 5 and 6); reports to the Chief Privacy Officer. Level of decision authority: operational decisions assigned line by line in the RACI Matrix — for example, the standard track of a risk calculation project, calculation of residual risk, PET application, DSR responses, the RoPA; non-standard projects go to the Chief Privacy Officer and the Privacy Risk Committee; no authority to accept residual risk.

| Requirement category | Requirement |
| :---- | :---- |
| Formal education | A university degree; a specialisation in data protection, risk management, or operational management |
| Mandatory | A recognised privacy programme-management certification or equivalent (example: IAPP CIPM) |
| Desirable | A recognised privacy-law certification or equivalent (example: IAPP CIPP/E) within the first year |
| Experience | At least 4 years of experience in the operational management of compliance or risk processes at a financial institution |
| Specialised competencies | The ability to determine whether a risk calculation project or processing request falls on the standard or non-standard track (RACI Matrix, Section 5.1) — for example, the launch of a new scoring feature (non-standard track) or a routine PEP configuration update (standard track); AI literacy (Section 4.8) |
| Independence | See Section 5 — limited form: a prohibition on holding, at the same time, a role in a business line whose decisions are assessed within a given process |

***4.3 Privacy Officer***

Is Responsible for performing DPIAs for scoring and anti-fraud models with the business, processing customer requests, maintaining the RoPA and vendor privacy due diligence at PanEuropean (RACI Matrix, Sections 5.2, 5.3 and 6); acts under the Group DPO's direction when performing monitoring tasks (row G6.1). Level of decision authority: none on residual risk; prepares analyses for the Privacy Operations Lead, the Processing Owner and General Counsel.

| Requirement category | Requirement |
| :---- | :---- |
| Formal education | A university degree; in law, economics, or a compliance-related field |
| Mandatory | A recognised privacy-law certification or equivalent demonstrable expertise within 6 months of appointment (example: IAPP CIPP/E, or a recognised national certification of the officer's country) |
| Experience | 2 years of experience in data protection or banking compliance, or a completed Privacy Operations Lead onboarding programme in the absence of experience |
| National-law competence | Working knowledge of the data protection law and supervisory practice of the officer's country — for example, the BDSG (Germany), the French Data Protection Act (France), the Italian Personal Data Protection Code (Italy), the LOPDGDD (Spain), the Polish Personal Data Protection Act (Poland) — and of national banking-secrecy and AML rules; professional proficiency in the country's official language |
| Specialised competencies | Working proficiency in PanEuropean's DPIA methodology as applied to credit scoring; the lawful bases and Art. 9 conditions relevant to the product line; AI literacy (Section 4.8) |
| Independence | See Section 5 — limited form: a prohibition on holding, at the same time, a role in a business line whose decisions are assessed within a given DPIA |

***4.4 Privacy Engineer***

Is Responsible for selecting and applying PET techniques on PEP, including developing/configuring new techniques, preparing risk calculation projects, and operating the platform (RACI Matrix, Sections 5.1, 5.2 and row S9). Level of decision authority: none on residual risk; performs the technical implementation and reports technical results — including any failed mandatory metric or known feasible attack — to the Privacy Operations Lead.

| Requirement category | Requirement |
| :---- | :---- |
| Formal education | A university degree in a technical field — computer science, data engineering, statistics, or a related area |
| Mandatory | A recognised privacy-engineering certification or equivalent demonstrable expertise (examples: IAPP CIPT, ISACA CDPSE, or an assessed portfolio of privacy-engineering work) |
| Tool proficiency | Internal PEP proficiency assessment run by the Privacy Operations Lead, passed before independent authorisation to configure PET techniques; no vendor-issued certificate is required |
| Experience | 3 years of hands-on experience in data engineering, including experience with pseudonymisation techniques and synthetic data |
| Specialised competencies | Statistical disclosure control and differential privacy (privacy unit, adjacency, mechanism and accountant, composition — NIST SP 800-226); re-identification and membership-inference testing and their limits; the distinction between pseudonymised data, anonymous information and synthetic data (Document 18); AI literacy (Section 4.8) |
| Independence | See Section 5 — limited form: a prohibition on holding, at the same time, a role responsible for developing the scoring or anti-fraud model whose residual risk the Privacy Engineer subsequently assesses |

***4.5 Data Protection Coordinator***

A distributed role in Credit Decisions, Digital Banking, and Marketing with a dual reporting line (the "DPC"; Organisational Design Strategy, Document 4, Section 7). Level of decision authority: none; the role serves as a communication channel between the business unit and PanEuropean's privacy function, not a decision point. Unlike the Processing Owner (Section 4.6) — also a role within the business unit, but assigned to its head and vested with delegated decision authority — the DPC never decides on residual risk.

| Requirement category | Requirement |
| :---- | :---- |
| Formal education | Not set separately from the requirements of the incumbent's primary position in the relevant unit |
| Mandatory | Internal certification under PanEuropean's training programme (Document 30); external certification is not mandatory |
| Experience | Not set separately; the role is held concurrently with the incumbent's primary position in the relevant unit |
| Independence | See Section 5 — the dual reporting line built into the role itself is offset by the role's lack of decision authority, not by a separate independence requirement |

Common baseline competency for all three coordinators: the ability to recognise a situation requiring escalation to the Privacy Operations Lead (including a possible personal data breach), and not to give customers or colleagues an independent assessment of the permissibility of processing. In addition to the baseline competency, each coordinator has a requirement specific to their unit:

| Coordinator | Unit specifics | Additional competency |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| DPC, Credit Decisions | Works within the credit-scoring and anti-fraud model scope based on PEP (RACI Matrix, Section 5.2) | A basic understanding of automated decision-making and the customer's Art. 22(3) rights (Privacy Policy, Section 8); the ability to recognise a change to scoring features that requires DPIA triage before deployment to production; AI literacy (Section 4.8) |
| DPC, Digital Banking | Supports the rollout of new digital products and personalisation features in the PanEuropean app | An understanding of the mandatory gates before deployment to production (PanEuropean Charter, Section 11); the ability to recognise a profiling feature at the product-planning stage, rather than after release |
| DPC, Marketing | Supports consent for marketing communications and profiling for personalised offers (RACI Matrix, Section 5.5) | An understanding of the consent collection and withdrawal mechanism and the limits of permissible profiling set by the DPIA results; the ability to distinguish a campaign that falls within the approved limits from one requiring a fresh assessment |

***4.6 Processing Owner***

Is Accountable for processing-level decisions of the business line within the Management Board's delegation (PanEuropean Charter, Section 8): launching or changing processing, owning the DPIA, Art. 22(3) human review where the line takes automated decisions, consent design and withdrawal for its processing, retention execution, engagement of processors, and acceptance of residual risk at the Low tier and, with the Chief Privacy Officer's concurrence, at the Medium tier (RACI Matrix, Sections 5–7). Designated by the Management Board on the Chief Privacy Officer's proposal; held by the head of Credit Decisions, Digital Banking, Marketing, Customer Service or Fraud Prevention, or by the CIO for IT-operated processing, or by their qualified, authorised deputy. Level of decision authority: Low — independently; Medium — with the Chief Privacy Officer's concurrence; High — proposes to the CRO (or to the Privacy Risk Committee where the Management Board's written delegation designates it), after G-36 has been evaluated and, where the Art. 36 test is met, after prior consultation with the HBDI; Critical — not acceptable. No decision before G-L has been passed. Where the decision departs from the Group DPO's advice, the reasons are recorded (RACI Matrix, Section 7).

| Requirement category | Requirement |
| :---- | :---- |
| Formal education | Not set separately from the requirements of the head-of-line or CIO position |
| Mandatory | Completion, on designation, of the Processing Owner briefing (Charter Section 8, the mandatory gates, the acceptance tiers and this profile), confirmed by a short assessment |
| Experience | Not set separately; the role is held concurrently with the incumbent's primary position, or by their qualified, authorised deputy |
| Specialised competencies | The ability to weigh the expected benefit of lawful processing (for example, improved scoring-model accuracy) against the residual risk to customers and to reach a reasoned decision within the delegated tier; an understanding that benefit never cures a G-L failure or replaces prior consultation; the ability to articulate written reasons when departing from the Group DPO's advice; for the Head of Credit Decisions and the Head of Customer Service, AI literacy for the credit-scoring model and the customer-service assistant (Section 4.8) |
| Independence | Not applicable by design — the compensating controls are G-L, the Group DPO's mandatory advice on every DPIA, recorded reasons for departures, the Chief Privacy Officer's concurrence at Medium, escalation of High risk to the CRO, and the Group DPO's right to escalate directly to the Management Board |

***4.7 Chief Privacy Officer (EPPO)***

Is Accountable for operating PanEuropean's privacy programme (RACI Matrix, Section 4 and most of Section 6); reports to the CRO; gives concurrence to Medium-tier acceptance and prepares prior consultations with the HBDI together with the Processing Owner. Level of decision authority: programme decisions assigned in the RACI Matrix; concurrence at Medium; no authority to accept High residual risk.

| Requirement category | Requirement |
| :---- | :---- |
| Formal education | A university degree in law, risk management or a related field |
| Mandatory | A recognised privacy programme-management certification or equivalent demonstrable expertise (example: IAPP CIPM), and working knowledge of the GDPR and the BDSG |
| Experience | At least 8 years in compliance, risk or privacy at a financial institution, including at least 3 years leading a cross-functional programme reported to a management board |
| Specialised competencies | The mandatory gates; the two-track risk model (Document 18); separation of GDPR breach notification to the HBDI from DORA ICT-incident reporting to the financial supervisor (Document 14); AI literacy and AI-governance interfaces (Section 4.8) |
| Independence | See Section 5 — may not be the Group DPO or the Chief Audit Executive |

***4.8 Cross-Role Requirement — AI Literacy***

All roles in Sections 4.1–4.7, and the DPC for Credit Decisions, complete PanEuropean's AI literacy module, which covers the credit-scoring model (high-risk under AI Act Annex III; high-risk obligations apply from 2 December 2027) and the customer-service assistant provided by Example AI Provider Inc. (fictional): how the systems work, their limitations, Art. 22 safeguards, and the interfaces with Model Risk / AI governance (Document 3, Section 3.5). The module supports the AI literacy measures referred to in Art. 4 of the AI Act, applied in the version in force.

## **5. Independence Requirements**

The independence requirements apply first and foremost to the Group DPO. The choice of the Group DPO's reporting line (direct reporting to the Management Board) is set out in the Organisational Design Strategy (Document 4, Section 7) — this section restates it as a requirement placed on the candidate and on the bank.

* A Group DPO candidate may not simultaneously hold a position within Data Governance, Digital Banking, Marketing or any other unit that determines the purposes and means of processing customer data — a direct application of WP243 rev.01, §3.5, which names the head of marketing and similar positions as typically conflicting with the DPO role; the assessment is recorded on designation and reviewed annually

* A Group DPO candidate must not have family or financial ties to the CDO, the CRO or the heads of the business lines that could create a conflict of interests

* The Group DPO does not report to the CDO or to any business-line head (Organisational Design Strategy, Document 4, Section 7); independence is in any case assessed on the merits through the annual conflict review, and Group Internal Audit may test it

* The Group DPO is not dismissed or penalised for performing its tasks (Art. 38(3)); as a mandatory DPO in Germany, the special dismissal protection of §38(2) with §6(4) BDSG applies. Any disciplinary or personnel measure concerning the Group DPO or the Deputy Group DPO is decided by the Management Board, not by an administrative superior alone, after the person concerned has been heard; where the Group DPO considers that a measure relates to the performance of its tasks, the chair of the Risk Committee of the Supervisory Board is informed. The content of advice, objections later shown to be unfounded, and escalations are excluded from any performance or disciplinary assessment. This safeguard does not bar measures taken on grounds unrelated to the performance of the DPO's tasks

* PanEuropean funds the Group DPO's and the Deputy Group DPO's certification upkeep and continuing professional education from the Group DPO office budget (Art. 38(2)); a lapse does not suspend either of them

* The Chief Privacy Officer may not be the Group DPO, and neither may be the Chief Audit Executive

* For the Privacy Operations Lead, Privacy Officer, and Privacy Engineer, the independence requirements apply in limited form — a prohibition on holding, at the same time, a role in a business line whose decisions or technical work they assess within a given process

* At PanEuropean, by design, the Data Protection Coordinator role is administratively part of the same unit (Credit Decisions, Digital Banking, or Marketing) whose decisions it helps assess — which is why the DPC is never vested with decision authority: the absence of authority (Section 4.5), not an independence requirement, is the compensating mechanism for this role

* By design, the Processing Owner (Section 4.6) does not possess independence from the business line whose decisions it takes — a deliberate feature of the role: the compensating mechanisms are G-L, the Group DPO's mandatory advice, recorded reasons for departures, the Chief Privacy Officer's concurrence at Medium, escalation of High risk to the CRO, and the Group DPO's right to escalate directly to the Management Board (PanEuropean Charter, Section 8)

## **7. Relationship to Other Documents in the Model**

This document is built on PanEuropean's RACI Matrix (Document 5) and turns the distribution of responsibility into a verifiable qualification requirement. This document:

* Relies on the roles set out in PanEuropean's RACI Matrix (Section 3) — it does not introduce new roles and does not redefine the R/A/D/C/I distribution

* Feeds PanEuropean's Privacy Awareness & Training Programme (Document 30) — the training programme is built to cover the competency requirements set out here, including the AI literacy module

* Is operationalised in PanEuropean's Operational Role & Staffing Model (Document 26) — there, the competency requirements are mapped against the assessment results of specific incumbent positions (for example, "Privacy Officer, Germany")

* The decision authority of the Processing Owner and the Chief Privacy Officer (Sections 4.6–4.7) follows the acceptance table in Section 7 of PanEuropean's RACI Matrix and its decision rows in Section 5.2; for other roles, authority follows the line-by-line allocation in Sections 4–6 of the RACI Matrix

* Relies on the mandates established by the PanEuropean Charter (Section 8) and on the reporting-line analysis in the Organisational Design Strategy (Document 4, Section 7)

# PGF-31 — Governance Reporting Template

*Privacy Governance Framework (PGF) · Level 4 — Control, Reporting and Improvement · Release 1.0.0-rc.2 · Regulatory reference date: 26 October 2026*

> **Status and use.** Part A is a reusable template. Part B is a worked example for a fictional organisation (PanEuropean Bank AG); the named implementation profile identifies a real product; the Bank, deployment, figures, dates and records in Part B are illustrative. Nothing in this document is legal advice or a statement about any real organisation, product or provider.

# **About This Document**

**Purpose.** Document 31 sets the structure, cadence and content rules of the privacy programme report (process G8.1) that the Executive Privacy Programme Owner (EPPO) presents to Executive Management and to the Governing Body's Risk Committee. It defines how the report relates to the DPO's own report to the highest management level (G8.2) and to the independent assurance opinion of Internal Audit (G6.2b), and it holds the framework's master escalation matrix (Section 6.4), which Documents 27–30 cite.

**Scope.** Selection, interpretation and presentation of facts already held in the framework — metrics (Document 28), risk records (Document 22), monitoring and audit results (Document 27), breaches and incidents (Document 14), review and training status (Documents 29, 30) — and the format of decision requests. It introduces no metric of its own.

**Relationships.** Document 28 is the only metric source; Document 5 (rows G6–G8) fixes the roles; Document 8 fixes who decides on residual risk and limits; Document 24 (Section 4.9) describes the reporting process.

## **1. Purpose of This Document**

This document is Document 31 of the Privacy Governance Framework (PGF), the fifth document of Level 4 "Control, Reporting and Improvement". It sets the structure and the at least ***[quarterly]*** cadence of the privacy programme report (G8.1): the mandatory sections (Section 5), scheduled and unscheduled submission (Section 6), the content rules (Sections 7–9) and the rules for aggregation, interpretation and the rationale of requested decisions (Section 11).

Three reporting lines are kept separate:

* **Programme report (G8.1)** — prepared by the ***[Privacy Operations Lead]***, owned and presented by the ***[EPPO]*** to ***[Executive Management]***, which decides on the management matters it raises, and to the ***[Risk Committee of the Governing Body]***, which oversees. This document governs it
* **DPO report (G8.2)** — the DPO's own report to the highest management level (GDPR Art. 38(3)), at least ***[annually]*** and ad hoc, and to the Governing Body committee where the DPO considers it necessary. It is separate, is not edited or approved by the EPPO, and is not governed by this document beyond the interface in Section 6.5
* **Independent assurance opinion (G6.2b)** — issued by ***[Independent Internal Audit]*** to the ***[Audit Committee]*** under its own charter (Document 27). The programme report summarises its status but neither filters nor restates it

The report presents existing facts — metrics, risk records, monitoring and audit results, incidents, the status of earlier decisions — in a form suitable for decisions. It does not introduce new metrics or redefine audit methodology.

## **2. Scope**

* ***[The mandatory structure and content of the programme report (Section 5), its scheduled and unscheduled submission (Section 6), and the rules for selecting metrics, risk records, assurance results, incidents and decision requests (Sections 7–9)]***
* ***[The master escalation matrix (Section 6.4) that sets addressees and time limits for the escalation levels used in Documents 27–31]***
* ***[The rules for aggregating and interpreting existing data and for tracking the implementation of decisions taken on earlier reports (Sections 9, 11)]***
* ***[Excludes: the metric catalogue and its calculation (Document 28); audit methodology and the independent opinion (Document 27); the setting of risk limits (Document 8); breach handling (Document 14); the meeting procedure of Executive Management and of the Governing Body committees; the content of the DPO's own report]***
* ***[Does not create a register: the sources remain Documents 22, 25, 27 and 28 and the records named there]***

## **3. Principles**

* ***[No new metrics: every number in the report carries a Document 28 identifier (CG-nn or M-…) or a record identifier from Document 22, 14 or 27; a needed metric that does not exist is first defined in Document 28]***
* ***[Interpretation, not raw data: trends, causes, rationale and recommendations; the dashboard remains available on request]***
* ***[Materiality in both directions: deviations outside target, High residual-risk records, material trends and significant achievements are included; unchanged green values are not listed line by line]***
* ***[Statutory events are never aggregated away: every missed statutory deadline in the period is listed by case, whatever the aggregate shows (Document 28, Section 10)]***
* ***[Right addressee: management decisions are requested of Executive Management or the decider designated in Document 8; the Governing Body committee receives information and exercises oversight, and is asked for consent only on matters reserved to it]***
* ***[Separate DPO channel: the programme report never substitutes for, summarises on the DPO's behalf, or filters the DPO's report; the DPO's right to escalate directly to Executive Management is unaffected (Art. 38(3))]***
* ***[Confidentiality: matters brought to the DPO are reported only in aggregate, without the content of a request or the identity of the requester (Art. 38(5)); breach and rights statistics are anonymised]***
* ***[Data-quality assertion: the report states the data cut-off, the sources and any metric published with a quality flag (Section 11)]***
* ***[Minimum cadence: the scheduled cycle is a minimum; unscheduled reports supplement it]***

## **6. Cadence, Triggers and the Master Escalation Matrix**

### **6.1 Scheduled Cycle**

| Stage | Timing | Responsible |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| ***[Draft report]*** | ***[No later than X business days after the period close]*** | ***[Privacy Operations Lead]*** |
| ***[DPO factual review of the passages on its tasks]*** | ***[X business days before approval]*** | ***[DPO]*** |
| ***[Content approval]*** | ***[No later than Y business days after the period close]*** | ***[EPPO]*** |
| ***[Presentation to Executive Management]*** | ***[At least quarterly]*** | ***[EPPO]*** |
| ***[Presentation to the Governing Body's Risk Committee]*** | ***[At the committee's next meeting, at least quarterly]*** | ***[EPPO; the responsible member of Executive Management attends]*** |

### **6.2 Unscheduled Triggers**

An unscheduled report supplements the scheduled cycle; it does not replace or postpone it. Time limits are those of the master escalation matrix (Section 6.4).

| Trigger | Source | Addressee and time limit |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| ***[A Critical-level item in any domain scale (Documents 27–31)]*** | ***[Document 27, Section 7; Document 28, Section 10; Document 29, Section 10; Document 30, Section 10; Section 10 of this document]*** | ***[Per Section 6.4, Critical]*** |
| ***[A High residual-risk decision; a prior consultation submitted; a written response or Art. 58 measure of the supervisory authority in a consultation]*** | ***[Documents 8, 19, 22]*** | ***[Executive Management (the decider is a member); Governing Body Risk Committee informed within X business days]*** |
| ***[A missed statutory deadline that concerns a supervisory-authority order, a prior consultation or a breach notification; several statutory misses with a common cause]*** | ***[Document 28: M-G4.1-01, M-S7-13, M-C3-02]*** | ***[Per Section 6.4, Critical]*** |
| ***[A personal data breach notified to the supervisory authority with severity at the top of the Document 14 scale; a major ICT-related incident with a privacy dimension]*** | ***[Document 14]*** | ***[Executive Management per Document 14; Governing Body Risk Committee informed within X business days]*** |
| ***[A corrective measure or fine of a supervisory authority]*** | ***[Document 28: M-G4.1-03]*** | ***[Per Section 6.4, Critical]*** |
| ***[A cumulative disclosure-budget limit or another hard limit of Document 8 reached or at risk]*** | ***[Document 28: M-G2-05]*** | ***[Per Section 6.4, Critical (limit exceeded) or High (warning level)]*** |

* ***[Several triggers arising close together may be combined in one unscheduled report at the EPPO's discretion, provided none requires immediate presentation on its own]***
* ***[The DPO's direct escalation to Executive Management is not an unscheduled trigger of this document: it is the DPO's own channel (Section 6.5) and is not routed through the EPPO]***

> **Guidance.** Not every unscheduled trigger needs an extra meeting. For triggers without a same-day time limit, a written report and the next scheduled meeting suffice, if delaying the meeting does not delay the response to the risk.

### **6.3 Additional Requests**

* ***[A question raised by Executive Management or the Governing Body committee beyond the report is minuted and answered by the EPPO within X business days, unless another deadline is set]***
* ***[The number and subject of such requests are tracked as metric M-G8-04 (Document 28), a completeness signal for the report — not a new metric of this document]***

### **6.4 Master Escalation Matrix**

This matrix is the single source of addressees and time limits for the escalation levels used in the framework's control documents: audit and monitoring non-conformities (Document 27, Section 7), metric deviations (Document 28, Section 10), missed review deadlines (Document 29, Section 10), training non-completion (Document 30, Section 10) and reporting-cycle non-conformance (Section 10 of this document). Each of those sections defines what counts as Low, Medium, High or Critical in its own domain; this matrix defines who is told and by when. Time limits run from detection; a shorter limit is always required for a higher level.

| Level | First addressee | Notification | Action required | Reporting |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Low | ***[Process or document owner]*** | ***[Within the normal cycle]*** | ***[Corrected within the normal cycle]*** | ***[Dashboard (Document 28)]*** |
| Medium | ***[Privacy Operations Lead]*** | ***[Within X business days]*** | ***[Remediation plan within X business days]*** | ***[Dashboard; programme report if unresolved at period end]*** |
| High | ***[EPPO]*** | ***[Within X business days]*** | ***[Remediation plan within X business days (shorter than Medium); the EPPO decides measures within its authority or escalates]*** | ***[Next programme report to Executive Management and the Governing Body Risk Committee]*** |
| Critical | ***[EPPO and the responsible member of Executive Management]*** | ***[Same business day]*** | ***[Executive Management decides measures within X business days]*** | ***[Chair of the Governing Body Risk Committee informed within X business days by an unscheduled report (Section 6.2); the Audit Committee chair where the item is an audit finding]*** |
| Statutory deadline missed or statutory duty breached (any number of cases) | ***[EPPO and Legal; the DPO informed]*** | ***[Within 1 business day of detection, per case]*** | ***[Immediate corrective action, including late fulfilment or notification with reasons where the law requires; issues-log entry (Document 27, Section 10); never treated as an accepted risk]*** | ***[Listed by case in the next programme report; Critical where several cases share a cause or the miss concerns a supervisory-authority order, a prior consultation or a breach notification]*** |

* ***[The matrix never delays a statutory clock: breach decisions follow Document 14 (Art. 33/34; DORA reports) and are not held back for escalation]***
* ***[The matrix does not restrict independent channels: the DPO may escalate directly to Executive Management and inform the Governing Body committee (Art. 38(3)); the Chief Audit Executive reports functionally to the Audit Committee (Document 27)]***
* ***[Individual training lapses and individual review delays are never Critical on their own; Critical is reserved for systemic failures or items that affect a legal duty or a Document 8 limit]***

### **6.5 Interface with the DPO's Report and the Internal Audit Opinion**

* ***[The DPO's report (G8.2) is delivered at least annually and ad hoc to Executive Management, and to the Governing Body committee where the DPO considers it necessary. The programme report records only that the DPO's report was delivered (M-G8-05) and lists any decision Executive Management took on it, with the DPO's agreement on wording]***
* ***[The Internal Audit opinion (G6.2b) goes from the Chief Audit Executive to the Audit Committee. The programme report shows the status of privacy engagements in the approved audit plan (M-G6-06), the date of the latest opinion, and management's remediation of audit findings (issues log)]***

## **7. Metrics and Trends**

* ***[Include Charter-goal metrics (CG) separately from operational metrics (M) — Executive Management owns the objectives; process owners own the operations]***
* ***[Include metrics outside target (amber) or outside tolerance (Document 28, Section 6.0), metrics at High or Critical level (Document 28, Section 10), material trends over two or more cycles, and significant achievements; there is no "red zone" — use the zones and levels of Document 28]***
* ***[List statutory metrics (type S) by case whenever any case was missed, even if the percentage is high]***
* ***[Compare each included metric with at least the two preceding periods where they exist; an isolated deviation is reported as such]***
* ***[Diagnostic metrics (type D) are shown only with their owner's analysis and never as performance against a target]***
* ***[A metric appearing for the first time after leaving the green zone is accompanied by its established cause, or the statement that the cause is under analysis]***

## **8. Risk Profile and Requested Decisions**

* ***[Include every current High record of track (a) in the Privacy Risk Register (Document 22) with: the single decider; the date and basis of the decision; the G-36 conclusion; any prior consultation (submission date, authority requests, status); conditions and expiry. A Critical record cannot exist as an accepted risk: if one appears, processing is stopped or not started and the item is escalated as Critical]***
* ***[Show records whose tier changed since the previous report, in either direction]***
* ***[Show open prior consultations separately. "Consultation submitted" or an elapsed period never means "approved"; processing waits for the consultation step and the decider's decision]***
* ***[Show transfers on hold (G-TIA incomplete) or suspended (failed G-TIA), with the next legal step]***
* ***[Show conditional acceptances expiring in the next quarter and cumulative disclosure-budget utilisation against the Document 8 limits]***
* ***[Show track (b) organisational consequences in a separate table; they are never netted against track (a)]***
* ***[A High record carried for more than one cycle without a change to its treatment plan is explained; repeating the previous entry is not sufficient]***
* ***[Neither the DPO nor the EPPO "accepts" a residual risk in the report. Acceptance is shown as taken by the decider designated under Document 8 (Processing Owner up to Medium, with EPPO concurrence at Medium; one named member of Executive Management for High), with the DPO's advice recorded]***

***[Format of a requested decision: (1) context — the fact or deviation that requires a decision; (2) options — at least two, including "no change"; (3) the recommendation and its rationale; (4) the DPO's advice where the matter has a significant data protection impact, and, for a residual-risk matter, the Processing Owner's position; (5) consequences of inaction by a stated date; (6) the competent decider — Executive Management or the decider designated under Document 8 for management matters; the Governing Body or its committee only for reserved consent matters or oversight]***

* ***[Typical decision requests to Executive Management: a change to the limits of Document 8 (with the Governing Body's consent where the limit is reserved to it); programme budget or staffing (Documents 4, 26); a time-limited policy exception (Document 29, Section 7); priorities where a limit is close to being reached]***
* ***[A requested decision not taken by its date stays on the decision implementation list, flagged "not decided"]***

## **9. Assurance, Incidents and Decision Implementation**

* ***[Monitoring: a summary of the DPO's monitoring (G6.1) in the form the DPO provides — number of activities, findings by level, issues-log status — without the content of individual contacts to the DPO]***
* ***[Internal audit: status of privacy engagements in the plan approved by the Audit Committee; the date and overall rating of the latest independent opinion as communicated to the Audit Committee; management's remediation status. The programme report does not restate or reinterpret the opinion]***
* ***[Issues log: G-L failures, missed statutory deadlines and findings, with owner and remediation date (Document 27, Section 10)]***
* ***[Incidents: personal data breaches recorded, notified to the supervisory authority and communicated to data subjects (M-S7-08, M-S7-09, with M-S7-03 to M-S7-05 and the per-case listing M-S7-13), and — separately — major ICT-related incidents reported to the financial supervisor (M-S7-10, M-S7-06); anonymised, without information identifying data subjects or employees]***
* ***[Decision implementation: each decision taken on an earlier report, with owner (Document 25), due date, status (not started / in progress / completed / not decided) and, for "completed", the implementation evidence (an updated document, a register entry, an evidence pack)]***

## **10. Escalation for Reporting-Cycle Non-Conformance**

This scale classifies the reporting cycle itself — timeliness and completeness. Addressees and time limits follow the master escalation matrix (Section 6.4).

| Level | Description |
| :---- | :---- |
| Low | ***[Draft on time; no completeness concerns]*** |
| Medium | ***[Draft up to X business days late, or substantive rework needed after the EPPO's review; the presentation date is kept]*** |
| High | ***[A mandatory section (Section 5) or a High/Critical item, or a statutory miss, missing from the draft and found before presentation]*** |
| Critical | ***[A scheduled report not presented at all; a missing High/Critical item or statutory miss found only after presentation; an interpretation found to contradict its source data (Section 11)]*** |

## **11. Aggregation, Interpretation and Decision Rationale**

* ***[No value is recalculated for the report: the source is the latest validated value in Document 28, the register record (Document 22) or the audit or monitoring record (Document 27) at the data cut-off; a discrepancy is corrected in the report, not in the source]***
* ***[A trend needs at least two preceding periods; otherwise the value is reported as an isolated observation]***
* ***[Selection by materiality is symmetrical: deviations and achievements alike]***
* ***[The rationale for each requested decision is drafted by the Privacy Operations Lead and personally reviewed by the EPPO before submission]***
* ***[Wording may not contradict source data for a more favourable presentation; a contradiction found after submission is a Critical reporting non-conformance (Section 10) and is corrected in writing]***
* ***[Each report ends with a data-quality statement: data cut-off; sources; metrics published with a quality or "not verified" flag; known limitations]***

## **1. Purpose of This Document**

This document applies the Governance Reporting Template to PanEuropean Bank AG as at 23 October 2026. It fixes the quarterly programme report (G8.1) of the Chief Privacy Officer to the Management Board — the controller's management body and highest management level — and to the Risk Committee of the Supervisory Board, which oversees. Appendix A contains the programme report for the third quarter of 2026.

Group Internal Audit's independent opinion goes from the Chief Audit Executive to the Audit Committee (Document 27). Neither report is edited by the Chief Privacy Officer.

## **2. Scope**

* The quarterly programme report: structure (Section 5), scheduled and unscheduled submission and the master escalation matrix (Section 6), and the rules for metrics, risk records, assurance, incidents and decision requests (Sections 7–9)
* Sources: the Privacy Metrics Dashboard (Document 28), the Privacy Risk Register (Document 22, the bank's "Conduct & Privacy Risk" extension of the enterprise risk register), the issues log and monitoring and audit records (Document 27), the breach record (Document 14)

## **3. Principles**

* No new metrics: for example, the consent withdrawal rate that the report may show is M-C5-05, and the count of additional questions from the Board is M-G8-04 — both defined in Document 28
* Statutory events are listed by case: any request answered after its statutory deadline, any late HBDI notification and any missed authority deadline appears in the report, whatever the quarterly percentage
* Right addressee: the Management Board decides management matters (or the Chief Risk Officer as the designated single decider for High residual risk, Document 8); the Risk Committee is informed and oversees; Supervisory Board consent is requested only for its three reserved matters (Charter adoption or material amendment; the board-level privacy risk appetite of Document 8, Section 3; removal of the Group DPO)
* Separate Group DPO channel: the Group DPO reviews passages on its tasks for factual accuracy and may attach a statement; its own report and its right of direct escalation to the Management Board are unaffected
* Confidentiality: contacts to the Group DPO and data subject requests appear only as counts

***6.2 Unscheduled Triggers***

PanEuropean applies the triggers of Part A, Section 6.2, with these specifics: a High residual-risk decision, a prior consultation submitted to the HBDI and any written response or measure of the HBDI in a consultation are reported to the Risk Committee within 5 business days (Document 8, Section 5); a personal data breach notified to the HBDI is reported to the Management Board under Document 14 and listed in the next programme report; a reached warning level of the cumulative disclosure budget (80% of a lifetime limit, Document 28) is a High item, and a planned release that would exceed a limit is a Critical item.

***6.3 Additional Requests***

Questions from the Management Board or the Risk Committee beyond the report are minuted and answered by the Chief Privacy Officer within 10 business days unless another date is set; their number and subject are tracked as M-G8-04.

***6.4 Master Escalation Matrix (PanEuropean values)***

| Level | First addressee | Notification | Action required | Reporting |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Low | Process or document owner | Normal cycle | Corrected within the normal cycle | Dashboard |
| Medium | Privacy Operations Lead | Within 5 business days | Remediation plan within 10 business days | Dashboard; programme report if unresolved |
| High | Chief Privacy Officer | Within 2 business days | Remediation plan within 5 business days | Next programme report to the Management Board and the Risk Committee |
| Critical | Chief Privacy Officer and Chief Risk Officer | Same business day | Management Board decision on measures within 5 business days | Chair of the Supervisory Board's Risk Committee informed within 2 business days; chair of the Audit Committee where the item is an audit finding |
| Statutory deadline missed or statutory duty breached | Chief Privacy Officer and General Counsel; Group DPO informed | Within 1 business day, per case | Immediate corrective action; issues-log entry | Listed by case in the next programme report; Critical where several cases share a cause or the miss concerns an HBDI order, a prior consultation or a breach notification |

***Decision Requested — Use of the Remaining δ Headroom***

* **Context —** the lifetime δ ledger for the retail customer population is at about 80% of the limit set in Document 8, Section 3. The remaining headroom (about 2×10⁻⁶) is needed for the recalibration of the credit-scoring training generator (DPIA-CS-TRAIN-01, re-assessment due by 25 March 2027 or at the next calibration) and for further rounds of the federated anti-fraud model (PRR-FL-01)
* **Options —** (1) no change: plan each differentially private release case by case against the remaining headroom; (2) the Management Board sets a priority order: reserve the remaining δ headroom for the credit-scoring recalibration and the federated-learning rounds, and require any other release from the retail customer population to use a method that does not draw on the ledger, or to wait for the next annual review of Document 8; (3) propose an amendment of the lifetime limit — a Supervisory Board reserved consent matter (Document 8, Section 3)
* **Recommendation —** option (2); option (3) would change the board-level appetite without a new privacy analysis; option (1) risks that a lower-priority release consumes the headroom and that PEP blocks the recalibration
* **Group DPO advice (19 October 2026) —** supports option (2); advises against option (3) unless a new analysis of composition across releases is presented
* **Consequences of inaction —** a lower-priority release could exhaust the headroom before March 2027, delaying the recalibration required by the expiry of DPIA-CS-TRAIN-01
* **Competent decider —** Management Board (allocation within an existing limit, process G2.1); the Risk Committee of the Supervisory Board is informed

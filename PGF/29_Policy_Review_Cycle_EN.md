# PGF-29 — Policy Review Cycle

*Privacy Governance Framework (PGF) · Level 4 — Control, Reporting and Improvement · Version 1.0.0 · Regulatory reference date: 26 October 2026*

> **Status and use.** Part A is a reusable template. Part B is a worked example for a fictional organisation (PanEuropean Bank AG); the named implementation profile identifies a real product; the Bank, deployment, figures, dates and records in Part B are illustrative. Nothing in this document is legal advice or a statement about any real organisation, product or provider.

# **About This Document**

**Purpose.** Document 29 sets the procedure by which all documents of the Privacy Governance Framework (PGF) are reviewed periodically and ad hoc, how changes are classified and approved, and how versions are controlled. It operationalises the continuous-improvement principle of the Charter (Document 2, Section 7) and the duty to review and update measures where necessary (GDPR Art. 24(1)).

**Scope.** Every document of the framework, including this one; for registers, their definitions and structure, not their records.

**Relationships.** The "Policies and Standards" process (Document 24, Section 4.6) applies this document; regulatory change reaches it through the Regulatory Applicability Matrix (Document 7, Sections 5.1 and 6); compliance with adopted documents is tested under Document 27; review metrics are defined in Document 28 (M-G5-05 to M-G5-07); escalation follows Document 31, Section 6.4.

## **1. Purpose of the Document**

This document is Document 29 of the Privacy Governance Framework (PGF), the third document of Level 4 "Control, Reporting and Improvement". It sets the procedure for the periodic and ad hoc review of policies and other framework documents: frequencies, grounds for ad hoc review, the classification and approval of changes, and versioning and document control.

Unlike the Privacy Audit and Monitoring Programme (Document 27), which asks whether the adopted model is followed in practice, this document asks whether the model is still right. The two loops complement each other: an outdated document that is followed is as serious a problem as a current document that is not.

## **2. Scope**

* All documents of the framework, from Level 1 to Level 4, including this document (Section 11)
* This document governs when and how a document is reviewed, approved and versioned — not its substantive content
* For registers (Documents 21–23), only the definitions, fields and structure are reviewed; records are maintained by their own processes
* For the process-model documents (Documents 24, 26), the frequency of Section 5 is a safety net; the primary basis for review is the event trigger stated in those documents
* Time-limited exceptions to a policy are within scope: they are recorded, approved and reviewed on expiry (Section 7)
* Does not replace Document 27 (Section 1)

## **3. Principles**

* **Minimum frequency.** Section 5 sets minimum frequencies; a document owner may review more often
* **Escalation by materiality.** A material change (to an obligation, requirement, threshold or allocation of responsibility) and a non-material wording change follow different approval routes (Section 7)
* **Management adopts; the DPO advises.** Privacy policies are adopted by ***[Executive Management]*** or its delegate unless the Governing Body has reserved a specific matter to its consent. The DPO gives mandatory advice (C*) on changes with data protection impact; it neither approves changes nor decides their materiality
* **No parallel version register.** Each document's own version history is the record (Section 8)
* **Traceable outcome.** Every review ends either in a new version or in a dated "reviewed, no changes required" entry, so that the absence of a new version can be told apart from a missed review
* **Authoritative language.** Each document states its authoritative language version; other language versions are translations, and a discrepancy is corrected visibly, not silently (Section 8)

## **8. Versioning and Document Control**

* ***[A version has a major and a minor digit (X.Y); the first published version is 1.0]***
* ***[A material change increments the major digit and resets the minor digit (1.3 → 2.0); a non-material change increments the minor digit (1.0 → 1.1)]***
* ***[Every version change is recorded in the version history of the document; this document keeps no central log]***
* ***[A scheduled review without changes is recorded as "reviewed, no changes required" with its date, without a new version number]***
* ***[Each version states its adoption date, effective date and publication date; superseded versions are withdrawn from use, marked as superseded and kept for the retention period of the organisation's record-keeping rules for governance documents; Document 11 applies to any personal data they contain]***
* ***[New or changed obligations for staff are communicated through targeted communications and, where needed, an attestation (Document 30)]***
* ***[Transitional rule: documents adopted before this document takes effect keep their version numbers; this section applies to their later changes without recalculating earlier history]***
* ***[Language versions: each document names its authoritative language version. A translation carries the version number of the authoritative version. A substantive discrepancy found in a translation is corrected as a minor version of the translation with a change notice to the staff who rely on it, because they may have acted on the wrong text. Where national law requires a language version for documents that impose obligations on employees, the organisation records which language version is legally operative in that country]***

## **6. Grounds for Ad Hoc Review**

An ad hoc review is initiated within 10 business days of the ground arising, unless a document sets a shorter period; for regulatory change, Document 7, Section 6 sets the assessment timeline. The period is the benchmark of Section 10 and of metric M-G5-06.

* A regulatory change assessed as applicable (Document 7, Section 6) — for example, the AI Omnibus (in force since 27 July 2026): high-risk obligations for Annex III systems such as credit scoring apply from 2 December 2027, which drives the planned fundamental-rights impact assessment for credit scoring (Document 19) and the AI-literacy content of Document 30; the date does not suspend any GDPR duty
* Pending changes in Document 7, Section 5.1 — for example, the Digital Omnibus: after the Council's negotiating mandate of 21 October 2026, General Counsel recorded it as a watch item; Documents 14 and 28 (72-hour clock, metric M-S7-04) are not revised while it is a proposal, and an impact assessment is due on adoption. Bill C-36 in Canada is tracked in the Part A template of Document 7 for organisations with Canadian operations; PanEuropean has none, so it is not a trigger here
* A supervisory finding or order — HBDI or a concerned authority; or the ECB / BaFin where a finding has a privacy dimension
* A material breach that revealed a gap (Document 14); under DORA Art. 6(5), a major ICT-related incident, a supervisory instruction, or a conclusion from digital operational resilience testing or audit
* A non-conformity from Group DPO monitoring or Group Internal Audit (Document 27), in particular a recurring or Critical one
* Document-specific triggers: Document 15 before the planned launch of automated credit decisions (1 February 2027) and when AI Act Art. 86 applies; Document 16 on completion of the first legacy review cycle (planned by 31 December 2027); Document 17 when the European Supervisory Authorities designate or de-designate a critical ICT third-party provider used by PanEuropean; Document 13 when the personalised-offers pilot is extended or advertising-partner placement is to be launched; Documents 15 and 19 when the HBDI's written advice on the credit-scoring consultation is received
* An organisational change (Document 4) — for example, entry into a country beyond the current five (Germany, France, Italy, Spain, Poland)
* A change to the Charter's objectives (Document 2, Section 6) or to Section 8 of the Charter
* The addition or removal of a process on the Process Map (Document 24)
* A change to the composition of the 31-document framework
* A revision of Document 8 with consequences for dependent documents

* Classification is proposed by the document owner with General Counsel; the Group DPO advises. Where the Group DPO disagrees, its advice and the reasons for not following it are recorded and the Management Board decides; the Group DPO may also escalate directly to the Management Board
* The Privacy Risk Appetite Statement (Document 8): every change, material or not, is adopted by the Management Board; changes to Section 3 (board-level appetite) also need the Supervisory Board's consent
* Documents that define the Group DPO's mandate, position or resources — the Charter (Section 8), Documents 4, 5 (Group DPO rows) and 6 — are adopted by the Management Board after consulting the Group DPO, never approved by the Chief Privacy Officer or the Group DPO alone
* Policy exceptions are approved by the decision-maker named in the policy (by default the Management Board or the Chief Privacy Officer under delegation), with Group DPO advice where they have data protection impact, recorded with an expiry date and reviewed before expiry. Example: the documented exception for production AML screening (Document 10) — Processing Owner for AML screening with the Chief Privacy Officer's concurrence and Group DPO advice, expiry 12 months

## **8. Versioning and Document Control**

* Versions X.Y; first version 1.0; material change → next major version; non-material → next minor version (for example, a hypothetical renaming of a regulator would move a document from 2.0 to 2.1)
* Every change is recorded in the document's own fictional document history; a review without changes is recorded as "reviewed, no changes required"
* Each version states adoption, effective and publication dates; superseded versions are withdrawn from the intranet, marked "superseded" and retained under the bank's record-keeping rules for governance documents
* Changed obligations for staff are communicated through targeted communications under Document 30
* Transitional rule: documents adopted before 1 June 2026 kept their version numbers; the re-baseline of 20 October 2026 produced new major versions under Section 7
* Language versions: the English version of each PanEuropean framework document is authoritative. Staff-facing material derived from it (for example, the baseline training module and the customer notices) exists in German, French, Italian, Spanish and Polish; a substantive discrepancy in a translation is corrected as a minor version of that translation with a change notice to affected staff. Where national law requires a local-language version of a document that imposes obligations on employees, the Privacy Officer for that country records which version is legally operative there

# PGF-15 — Data Subject Rights Policy

*Privacy Governance Framework (PGF) · Level 2 — Policies and Standards · Release 1.0.0-rc.1 · Regulatory reference date: 26 October 2026*

> **Status and use.** Part A is a reusable template. Part B is a worked example for a fictional organisation (PanEuropean Bank AG); all names, figures, dates and records in Part B are illustrative. Nothing in this document is legal advice or a statement about any real organisation, product or provider.

# **About This Document**

**Purpose.** Document 15 governs the operational fulfilment of data subject rights under GDPR Arts. 12–22: which right applies to which processing, what each response must contain, the identity-verification loop, the time limits by request type, restrictions and grounds for refusal (including anti-money-laundering restrictions, the rights of others and national restrictions under Art. 23), automated decisions, the confidentiality of requests addressed to the DPO, and the roles involved.

**Scope.** Requests from data subjects (customers, employees, applicants, representatives acting for them) to the organisation as controller. Where the organisation acts as processor for a client, it assists that controller under the contract (Document 17); the controller answers the request.

**Relationships.** Document 1 (Privacy Policy and Notice) publishes the rights to data subjects — this document adds the operational layer; Document 11 — retention grounds, legal hold, erasure cascade and the restricted state; Document 13 — withdrawal of consent; Document 14 — communication of a personal data breach (Art. 34); Document 17 — processors' assistance and recipients; Document 19, Section 4.10 — the Art. 22 module of the DPIA; Document 5, Section 5.3 — roles in core process C3.

Part A is a template. Text in ***[square brackets, bold italic]*** is a fill-in or illustrative placeholder. Blocks marked **Guidance** are removed from the adopted version. Part B shows the template completed for the fictional PanEuropean Bank AG.

## **1. Purpose**

This document governs the operational fulfilment of data subject rights: the applicability of each right, the content of responses, the identity-verification loop, time limits by request type, restrictions and grounds for refusal, and the roles involved in handling requests.

## **2. Scope and Applicability of Rights by Lawful Basis**

* ***[This document applies whatever the lawful basis of processing (Document 1, table of purposes and lawful bases), but not every right applies to every basis. The applicability of each right is checked against the lawful basis of the specific processing recorded in the RoPA (Document 21), not presumed.]***

| Right | Applies to | Does not apply / limited |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| ***[Withdrawal of consent (Art. 7(3))]*** | ***[Processing based on consent or explicit consent (Document 13)]*** | ***[Processing on any other basis — there is no consent to withdraw]*** |
| ***[Objection on grounds relating to the person's particular situation (Art. 21(1))]*** | ***[Processing based on Art. 6(1)(e) (public task) or Art. 6(1)(f) (legitimate interests), including profiling based on those provisions]*** | ***[Processing based on contract, legal obligation or consent; the controller may continue where it demonstrates compelling legitimate grounds that override the person's interests, rights and freedoms, or for legal claims]*** |
| ***[Objection to direct marketing (Art. 21(2)–(3))]*** | ***[Direct marketing on any basis, including related profiling]*** | ***[No exception: once the person objects, the data are no longer processed for direct marketing]*** |
| ***[Objection to research or statistics (Art. 21(6))]*** | ***[Processing for scientific or historical research or statistical purposes under Art. 89(1)]*** | ***[Unless necessary for a task carried out in the public interest]*** |

* ***[**Boundary with Document 13.** Document 13 defines the withdrawal mechanism in the organisation's own channels (for example, a toggle in the app); this document governs a withdrawal received through other channels (telephone, letter, e-mail) and its time limit.]***

* ***[**Communication of a personal data breach (Art. 34).** Data subjects are informed of a personal data breach likely to result in a high risk to their rights and freedoms under the Data Breach Response Policy (Document 14, Section 6), which governs its content, channel and timing. This is not the "right to be informed" of Arts. 13–14. This document provides the channel for follow-up requests after a communication (for example, a request for further information on the measures taken), handled with the standard time limit.]***

* ***[**Representatives.** Requests made by a representative (for example, a lawyer, a holder of parental responsibility, a guardian, or an heir where national law gives rights in respect of a deceased person) are accepted on proof of authority proportionate to the request; the person's own interests are respected (for example, a minor's views where appropriate).]***

## **3. Rights, Response Content and Grounds for Refusal**

> **Guidance.** The list of rights is published in Document 1 (01B, Section 12). This section adds the content each response must carry and the grounds on which a right may be limited. Reconcile the wording with Document 1 at every revision.

***3.1 Access (Art. 15)***

***[An access response contains, unless a restriction of Section 3.6 applies:]***

| Element | Content | Source |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| ***[Confirmation]*** | ***[Whether personal data concerning the person are processed]*** | ***[Art. 15(1)]*** |
| ***[Copy]*** | ***[A faithful and intelligible reproduction of all the personal data undergoing processing; where needed for the person to understand the data and exercise their rights effectively, copies of extracts from documents, entire documents or extracts from databases containing the data]*** | ***[Art. 15(3); CJEU C-487/21 *F.F. (Österreichische Datenschutzbehörde and CRIF)*, 4 May 2023]*** |
| ***[Purposes; categories of data]*** | ***[As recorded in the RoPA]*** | ***[Art. 15(1)(a)–(b)]*** |
| ***[Recipients]*** | ***[The identity of the actual recipients to whom the data have been or will be disclosed; categories of recipients only where it is impossible to identify them or the request is manifestly unfounded or excessive]*** | ***[Art. 15(1)(c); CJEU C-154/21 *Österreichische Post*, 12 January 2023]*** |
| ***[Retention period or criteria]*** | ***[From the retention matrix (Document 11)]*** | ***[Art. 15(1)(d)]*** |
| ***[Rights and complaint]*** | ***[Rectification, erasure, restriction, objection; the right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority]*** | ***[Art. 15(1)(e)–(f)]*** |
| ***[Source]*** | ***[Any available information on the source where data were not collected from the person]*** | ***[Art. 15(1)(g)]*** |
| ***[Automated decisions]*** | ***[The existence of decisions under Art. 22(1) and (4) and meaningful information about the logic involved, the significance and the envisaged consequences (Section 3.5)]*** | ***[Art. 15(1)(h); CJEU C-203/22]*** |
| ***[Transfers]*** | ***[Where data are transferred to a third country or international organisation, the appropriate safeguards (Art. 46)]*** | ***[Art. 15(2); Document 12]*** |
| ***[Consultations of the data]*** | ***[Where requested, information on consultation operations of the person's data — their dates and purposes — as information to which the person is entitled; the identity of the employees who carried them out is not provided as a rule, unless it is essential for the person to exercise their rights effectively, taking into account the employees' rights]*** | ***[CJEU C-579/21 *Pankki S*, 22 June 2023]*** |

* ***[**First copy free.** The first copy is provided free of charge, whatever the reason for the request — the person need not give reasons, and a request motivated by a purpose unrelated to data protection (for example, preparing litigation) is not refused for that reason (CJEU C-307/22 *FT (Copies du dossier médical)*, 26 October 2023). A reasonable fee based on administrative costs may be charged only for further copies (Art. 15(3)).]***

* ***[**Search.** The search covers all systems and records in which the person's data are held, including unstructured data, archives in the restricted state and data held by processors (EDPB Guidelines 01/2022). Record how data held only in backups are handled.]***

* ***[**Layered response.** Large responses are structured (for example, by product or system) and may be provided electronically by default where the request was made electronically (Art. 12(3)).]***

***3.2 Rectification, Erasure, Restriction and Notification of Recipients (Arts. 16–19)***

* ***[**Rectification (Art. 16).** Inaccurate data are rectified, and incomplete data completed, taking into account the purposes; a supplementary statement may be added. Where accuracy is contested, the data may be restricted while it is verified (below).]***

* ***[**Erasure (Art. 17).** Data are erased where a ground of Art. 17(1) applies — for example, the data are no longer necessary, consent is withdrawn and there is no other basis, a successful objection, or unlawful processing. Exceptions under Art. 17(3) — for example a legal obligation to retain (Document 11) or the establishment, exercise or defence of legal claims — are stated with the specific ground. Where the organisation has made the data public, it takes reasonable steps to inform controllers processing them of the request (Art. 17(2)). Erasure is propagated along the data lineage to derived personal data — pseudonymised copies, feature stores and synthetic datasets for which no anonymity conclusion has been made — under the erasure cascade of Document 11, Section 3.5, without undue delay and by a stated deadline that keeps the response within the time limit of Section 5; batch or scheduled runs never extend that time limit. For models and synthetic datasets, the Processing Owner first records, on Legal's assessment, a contextual conclusion on whether the current artefact still contains or allows the extraction of the person's data; where erasure is required, the artefact is restricted in the meantime and removed, regenerated or retrained by the deadline, and exclusion from the next run alone is used only where the recorded conclusion justifies it. Data kept under an exception enter the restricted state (Document 11, Section 4.1).]***

* ***[**Restriction (Art. 18(1)).** Processing is restricted where: (a) the person contests the accuracy of the data, for a period enabling the organisation to verify it; (b) the processing is unlawful and the person opposes erasure and requests restriction instead; (c) the organisation no longer needs the data, but the person requires them for the establishment, exercise or defence of legal claims; or (d) the person has objected under Art. 21(1), pending verification whether the organisation's legitimate grounds override those of the person. Restricted data are stored only, except with the person's consent, for legal claims, for the protection of another person's rights or for important public interest (Art. 18(2)); the person is informed before the restriction is lifted (Art. 18(3)).]***

* ***[**Notification of recipients (Art. 19).** Each rectification, erasure or restriction is communicated to every recipient to whom the data have been disclosed, unless this proves impossible or involves disproportionate effort; the person is told about those recipients on request. Recipients that are independent controllers apply the request to their own copy under their own responsibility (Document 17).]***

***3.3 Portability (Art. 20)***

***[Portability covers data the person provided — including data observed from the person's use of the service, such as transaction history and usage logs — processed by automated means on the basis of consent or contract. Data inferred or derived by the organisation (for example, an internal score) are excluded. Data are provided in a structured, commonly used and machine-readable format and, where technically feasible, transmitted directly to another controller (Article 29 Working Party Guidelines on the right to data portability, WP242 rev.01). Portability does not require the organisation to erase the data or to keep them longer.]***

***3.4 Objection (Art. 21)***

* ***[**Situational objection (Art. 21(1)).** For processing based on Art. 6(1)(e) or (f), including profiling, the organisation stops unless it demonstrates compelling legitimate grounds that override the person's interests, rights and freedoms, or the processing is needed for legal claims. The assessment is individual and recorded; processing is restricted while it is carried out (Art. 18(1)(d)).]***

* ***[**Direct marketing (Art. 21(2)–(3)).** Absolute: the data are no longer processed for direct marketing, including related profiling. There is no ground for refusal.]***

* ***[**Information.** The rights of Art. 21(1) and (2) are brought explicitly to the person's attention, clearly and separately from other information, at the latest at the time of the first communication (Art. 21(4); Document 13).]***

***3.5 Automated Decisions (Art. 22)***

* ***[**When Art. 22 applies.** A decision based solely on automated processing, including profiling, which produces legal effects or similarly significantly affects the person. An automatically produced score can itself be such a decision where it plays a determining role in a decision taken by a third party or by a human who does not exercise genuine influence (CJEU C-634/21 *SCHUFA Holding (Scoring)*, 7 December 2023).]***

* ***[**Exception and necessity record.** The exception relied on — Art. 22(2)(a) contract necessity, (b) Union or Member State law with suitable safeguards, or (c) explicit consent — and, for (a), the necessity record are documented in the DPIA's Art. 22 module (Document 19, Section 4.10). This document operates the safeguards; it does not decide whether the exception applies.]***

* ***[**Safeguards (Art. 22(3)).** At least the right to obtain human intervention on the part of the controller, to express one's point of view and to contest the decision. The review is carried out by staff with the authority and competence to change the decision (Document 5, Section 5.3); the Processing Owner is accountable.]***

* ***[**Explanation (Arts. 13(2)(f), 14(2)(g), 15(1)(h)).** Meaningful information about the logic involved: the procedure and principles actually applied, described concisely and intelligibly so that the person can understand which personal data were used and how — for example, the main factors that led to the result and what change in them would have led to a different result. The protection of trade secrets or of third parties' data may limit the disclosure of specific parameters; it does not remove the duty to explain, and where protected information is at stake it is handled as the applicable law provides, for example by communication to the supervisory authority or court for balancing (CJEU C-203/22 *Dun & Bradstreet Austria*, 27 February 2025).]***

* ***[**AI Act (forthcoming).** For decisions based on the output of certain high-risk AI systems, the AI Act (Art. 86) adds a right to a clear and meaningful explanation of the role of the AI system and the main elements of the decision, applying on the dates recorded in Document 7. It complements and does not replace Art. 22 GDPR.]***

* ***[**Sector rules.** Record any sector-specific rights that apply in parallel — for example, consumer-credit rules on human intervention and explanation of a creditworthiness assessment (Document 7).]***

***3.6 Restrictions and Refusals***

> **Guidance.** A refusal or restriction is a decision taken for the controller (Section 7) on Legal's assessment, with DPO advice where the case is disputed or novel. It is never taken by IT Management, and the DPO does not decide it.

| Ground | Rule |
| :---- | :---- |
| ***[Manifestly unfounded or excessive requests (Art. 12(5))]*** | ***[The organisation may charge a reasonable fee or refuse to act; it bears the burden of demonstrating the character of the request. Repetition alone is a trigger for review, not a ground: the assessment considers the intervals, whether the data have changed and any abusive intention (compare CJEU C-416/23 *Österreichische Datenschutzbehörde*, 9 January 2025, on requests to a supervisory authority). The first copy is always free (Section 3.1)]*** |
| ***[Rights and freedoms of others (Art. 15(4))]*** | ***[Data of other persons in the copy — for example, counterparties of transactions, employees, other customers — are redacted or withheld where disclosure would adversely affect their rights, including confidentiality owed under professional secrecy; the result is not a refusal of the whole request]*** |
| ***[Anti-money-laundering restrictions]*** | ***[Under applicable AML law, the organisation does not disclose that a suspicious-transaction report has been or will be made or that an analysis is being carried out (prohibition of "tipping off"). Data related to such reports or analyses are withheld, and the response is drafted so as not to reveal their existence; the AML function is consulted on every access request that touches flagged customers]*** |
| ***[Restrictions under Union or Member State law (Art. 23)]*** | ***[National restrictions of the rights (for example, for the prevention, investigation and prosecution of criminal offences, the protection of the person or others, or the enforcement of civil-law claims) are applied only as the national law provides, with the reasons recorded (Document 7)]*** |
| ***[Identification (Art. 11(2); Art. 12(2))]*** | ***[Where the organisation demonstrates that it is not in a position to identify the person, it informs the person and need not act, unless the person provides additional information enabling identification]*** |
| ***[Erasure exceptions (Art. 17(3)); legal hold]*** | ***[Statutory retention (Document 11) or a scoped legal hold for litigation, a supervisory investigation or a statutory request (Document 11, Section 7). The data enter the restricted state; the person is informed of the reason, unless this would prejudice the proceedings]*** |
| ***[Portability out of scope (Art. 20)]*** | ***[Processing not based on consent or contract, not automated, or data inferred by the organisation]*** |

***[Every refusal, partial refusal or restriction is communicated without delay and at the latest within one month of receipt, with the reasons, and with information on the possibility of lodging a complaint with a supervisory authority and of seeking a judicial remedy (Art. 12(4); Arts. 77–79), unless and to the extent that a restriction prohibits stating the reasons.]***

***3.7 Requests and Complaints Addressed to the DPO***

***[Data subjects may contact the DPO on all issues related to the processing of their data and the exercise of their rights (Art. 38(4)). The DPO is bound by secrecy or confidentiality concerning the performance of its tasks (Art. 38(5)). Where the EPPO, the Privacy Operations Lead or anyone else appears as "Informed" or "Consulted" on such a contact, this never means automatic disclosure of the content of the request or of the requester's identity: the DPO decides what is passed on, on a need-to-know basis and for the purpose of resolving the matter, using redaction or aggregate reporting where appropriate (WP243 rev.01, Section 2.6). Where the contact is also a rights request, the DPO forwards to the rights process only what is needed to fulfil it, with the person's knowledge. This does not prevent the DPO from investigating the matter or discussing it with management.]***

## **4. Requester Identity Verification**

* ***[**Proportionality.** Verification is proportionate to the sensitivity of the request and the class of data affected (Document 10): a request made from an authenticated session usually needs no further check; requests through other channels are verified against data the organisation already holds. A copy of an identity document is requested only where that is necessary and proportionate (EDPB Guidelines 01/2022).]***

* ***[**Identity loop, not rejection (Art. 12(6)).** Where the organisation has reasonable doubts about the identity of the requester, it requests the additional information necessary to confirm identity, without undue delay and specifying what is needed. It does not reject the request because of the doubt. If the information is not provided, the organisation informs the requester that it cannot act until identity is confirmed and how to resubmit (Art. 12(2), 11(2)).]***

* ***[**No over-collection.** Information requested for verification is used only for verification and deleted when it is no longer needed for that purpose.]***

* ***[**Withdrawal of consent.** Because a withdrawal only stops future processing and discloses nothing, verification for a withdrawal received outside the channels of Document 13 is carried out within the same interaction, and fulfilment is not deferred pending documents.]***

## **5. Time Limits by Request Type**

> **Guidance.** The statutory time limit is stated in Document 1 (01B, Section 12); this section breaks it down by request type and adds internal targets. Statutory deadlines are hard limits: a missed deadline is a breach of obligation and is escalated, even if an aggregate metric is green.

* ***[**Statutory rule (Art. 12(3)).** Information on action taken is provided without undue delay and in any event within one month of receipt of the request. The period may be extended by two further months where necessary, taking into account the complexity and number of the requests; the person is informed of any extension, with the reasons, within one month of receipt.]***

* ***[**When identity information is awaited.** Supervisory-authority practice on whether the period runs while the organisation awaits information requested under Art. 12(6) is not uniform. The organisation records the dates of the request, of its request for information and of the reply in the rights-request log; [Legal] records in Document 7 the approach applied in each jurisdiction; where that approach is unclear, the organisation plans the response so that it meets the period counted from receipt of the request.]***

## **6. Status Relative to IT Management and Business Units**

***[Technical extraction, rectification, restriction or erasure of data in specific systems is performed by the [Data Owners / IT Management] at the request of the Privacy Operations Lead, within the responsibilities set by the RACI Matrix (Document 5, Section 5.3). ## **1. Purpose**

This document governs the fulfilment of the rights of PanEuropean Bank AG's customers, applicants and other data subjects across the five countries of presence.

## **2. Scope and Applicability of Rights by Lawful Basis**

Applicability follows the table of purposes and lawful bases in the Customer Privacy Notice (Document 1, Section 7):

* **Withdrawal of consent** — e-mail and push marketing, personalised offers, PPI health data and the app's biometric log-in (Document 13, Section 2). There is nothing to withdraw for customer due diligence (legal obligation), the account and payments (contract), creditworthiness assessment (legal obligation) or fraud prevention (legitimate interests).
* **Situational objection (Art. 21(1))** — processing based on legitimate interests: fraud prevention and transaction monitoring (except where AML law requires monitoring), reporting to EuroCredit Bureau, anti-fraud matching with the partner bank, the federated-learning consortium, model development, and branch video surveillance. It does not apply to processing required by law.
* **Objection to direct marketing** — at any time, without exception (Section 3.4).
* **Communication of a personal data breach** — under Document 14, Section 6; follow-up requests are handled here with the standard time limit.
* **Channels.** Privacy requests: "Privacy settings" in the app, privacy@paneuropean-bank.example or post (privacy operations). Human review of credit decisions: "Request a Review" in the app or credit-review@paneuropean-bank.example (Credit Decisions). Contacts and complaints to the Group DPO: dpo@paneuropean-bank.example (Group DPO and Deputy Group DPO), handled under Section 3.7.

## **3. Rights, Response Content and Grounds for Refusal**

The rights are published in the Customer Privacy Notice (Document 1, Section 12). PanEuropean treats restriction and objection separately, as Part A does.

***3.1 Access***

The access response pack is assembled from the core banking platform, the customer-relationship system, card systems, credit-decision records, chat records (kept 12 months), PPI health data where held, EuroCredit Bureau data received by PanEuropean, CMS consent history and archives in the restricted state, together with data held by processors (Example Cloud Services Pvt Ltd holds only tokenised logs; Example AI Provider Inc. keeps any prompts and outputs for at most 30 days under its contract, and receives no data while the assistant's transfer is suspended (Document 12, Section 5) — both fictional, illustrative terms). It contains every element of Part A, Section 3.1, and in particular:

* **Recipients by name:** EuroCredit Bureau (independent controller), the PPI insurer (separate controller), the core banking platform provider, Example Cloud Services Pvt Ltd and Example AI Provider Inc. (processors), the partner bank for anti-fraud matching where the customer's identifier was part of a session (independent controller), and public authorities to which disclosure was made, except where a restriction of Section 3.6 applies.
* **Consultation log:** on request, the dates and purposes of consultations of the customer's records by PanEuropean staff, from the access logs; staff identities are not disclosed unless the customer shows that this is essential to exercise their rights, and then only after considering the staff members' rights.
* **Credit decisions:** the main factors used and their effect (Document 1, Section 8), and EuroCredit Bureau data and score as received.
* **First copy free.** Further copies within 12 months may incur a fee based on administrative costs, decided case by case; no fee is charged for any copy requested in connection with a complaint.
* **Search.** All live systems, archives and processors are searched. Backups (overwritten within at most 90 days, Document 11) normally duplicate live data; where data exist only in a backup, General Counsel decides how they are retrieved and the reason is recorded.

***3.2 Rectification, Erasure, Restriction and Notification of Recipients***

* **Rectification.** Where a customer contests EuroCredit Bureau data held by PanEuropean, the data are restricted under Art. 18(1)(a) while Credit Decisions verifies them with the bureau; the customer is also told that the bureau, as an independent controller, answers for its own records.
* **Erasure.** Account and contract data are erased at the end of the relationship, except records that statutory retention requires — accounting-relevant records for the statutory accounting retention period under applicable commercial and tax law, and AML/KYC records under the current German rule (GwG §8(4): five years from the end of the calendar year in which the business relationship ended) or the corresponding branch-country rule (Document 11, Section 5). Those records enter the restricted state. Erasure is executed off-cycle, without waiting for the monthly reconciliation run (Document 11, Section 3.5): the Privacy Operations Lead propagates it through PEP's lineage records to pseudonymised extracts and feature stores within 5 business days; for the synthetic calibration dataset and its generator, the Head of Credit Decisions records within 5 business days, on Legal & Compliance's assessment with Group DPO advice, whether the current artefacts still contain or allow the extraction of the customer's data — where erasure is required, the artefacts are restricted at once and regenerated or retrained without the customer within 30 days; in every case the customer is excluded from every subsequent run (illustrative internal deadlines, all within the response period of Section 5). The app's biometric template is stored only on the customer's device and is deleted by the app when the customer switches biometric log-in off; PanEuropean holds no server-side template, so there is no server-side erasure step.
* **Restriction.** Applied on any of the four grounds of Art. 18(1); the Privacy Operations Lead applies the flag in the systems concerned (Document 11, Section 4.1); the customer is told before a restriction is lifted.
* **Notification of recipients.** Rectifications, erasures and restrictions are passed to EuroCredit Bureau (for data PanEuropean reported to it), to the PPI insurer (for PPI data) and to the processors holding the data; the customer is told who these recipients are on request.

***3.3 Portability***

Covered: data the customer provided under the contract or consent, including observed data such as transaction history and app usage records. Excluded: data PanEuropean infers or derives, such as its internal credit score.

***3.4 Objection***

Objections to processing based on legitimate interests are assessed individually by the Privacy Operations Lead with the Processing Owner concerned (for example, the Head of Fraud Prevention for fraud monitoring); processing is restricted during the assessment; where PanEuropean relies on compelling legitimate grounds, the response explains them. Objections to direct marketing are executed immediately through the CMS (Document 13, Section 6).

***3.5 Automated Decisions***

* **Credit decisions (planned).** At 26 October 2026, credit decisions are taken by credit staff. Automated credit decisions for unsecured consumer loans requested in the app are planned for launch on 1 February 2027 (planned), only after the prior consultation of the HBDI — submitted on 15 September 2026 and pending — and the Chief Risk Officer's decision (Document 1, Section 8; DPIA-CS-INFER-01). In the planned design, under the Art. 22(2)(a) necessity record of DPIA-CS-INFER-01 (Document 19, Section 4.10), clear approvals and clear refusals are decided automatically, while scores in the borderline band are decided by a credit officer before any decision takes effect; every applicant can obtain human review.
* **Human review.** Through "Request a Review" or credit-review@paneuropean-bank.example; credit officers authorised to change the decision review it, consider the applicant's point of view and any additional information, and confirm or change the decision; the Head of Credit Decisions is accountable. Internal target: five business days.
* **Explanation.** On request and in every review, the applicant receives the main factors that led to the result, how they were weighed in general terms and what change would have led to a different result (C-203/22). Model parameters are not disclosed; this limits model-inversion risk but does not limit the explanation. Where the decision draws strongly on the EuroCredit Bureau score, the applicant is told that the bureau, as an independent controller, must also answer their rights in respect of the score (C-634/21).
* **Consumer credit and AI Act.** From 20 November 2026 (scheduled), consumer-credit rules applying Directive (EU) 2023/2225 also give the right to request human intervention and a clear explanation of a creditworthiness assessment involving automated processing (Document 1, Section 8). The AI Act right to explanation (Art. 86) for the high-risk credit-scoring system is forthcoming on the dates recorded in Document 7; Credit Decisions will provide it through the same review channel.
* **Fraud-suspicion blocking.** The transaction-monitoring system may block a payment or restrict a card or account automatically (Art. 22(2)(a); Document 1, Section 8). The customer is notified immediately unless the law prohibits it, may ask for human review through the contact centre or "Request a Review", and, once a payment is confirmed as legitimate, the block is lifted within 24 hours unless the law requires otherwise. The Head of Fraud Prevention is accountable for the review.

***3.6 Restrictions and Refusals***

| Ground | PanEuropean application |
| :---- | :---- |
| Manifestly unfounded or excessive | More than three substantively identical requests from the same customer within 12 months with no new circumstances trigger a review by the Privacy Operations Lead; the count is not itself a ground. A fee or refusal requires a written justification on General Counsel's assessment, and the customer is told the reason |
| Rights of others (Art. 15(4)) | Data of transaction counterparties, other customers and staff in documents are redacted where disclosure would adversely affect them, including banking confidentiality owed to other customers under national law |
| AML restrictions | Under applicable AML law, data relating to suspicious-transaction reports or ongoing analyses are withheld and the response does not reveal their existence; the AML Officer is consulted on every request concerning a flagged customer (Document 5, Section 5.3) |
| National restrictions (Art. 23) | Applied only as the national law of the country concerned provides (for Germany, the restrictions in the Federal Data Protection Act), with General Counsel's recorded reasons (Document 7) |
| Erasure exceptions; legal hold | Statutory retention (Section 3.2) or a scoped legal hold issued by General Counsel for litigation, an investigation by the HBDI or another data protection authority, or a financial-supervisory examination by the ECB or BaFin (Document 11, Section 7) |
| Portability out of scope | Inferred or derived data, such as the internal credit score |

Every refusal or restriction is communicated within one month of receipt with the reasons (unless a restriction prohibits stating them), the right to complain to the HBDI or to the supervisory authority of the Member State of the customer's habitual residence, place of work or place of the alleged infringement, and the right to a judicial remedy.

***3.7 Requests and Complaints Addressed to the Group DPO***

Contacts to dpo@paneuropean-bank.example are handled by the Group DPO or the Deputy Group DPO. The Group DPO decides what is passed to the Privacy Operations Lead to resolve a matter, on a need-to-know basis; the Chief Privacy Officer receives quarterly aggregate figures (number of contacts by topic and outcome) without requester identities, unless the Group DPO decides otherwise in a specific case. A complaint about how a rights request was handled is investigated by the Group DPO independently of the rights process.

## **4. Requester Identity Verification**

Requests from an authenticated app session need no further check, except that a request for a copy of PPI health data or a full portability export requires step-up authentication (re-entry of the app PIN) immediately before release, because releasing health data or a complete transaction history to a hijacked session would cause more harm than releasing ordinary contact data. Requests by e-mail or post are verified against data the Bank already holds (date of birth, recent account transactions), without a copy of an identity document unless reasonable doubt remains. Where doubt remains, Privacy Officers request the specific additional information within three business days of receipt (Art. 12(6)); the request is not rejected. Withdrawals of consent by telephone are verified within the same call (for example, date of birth and the most recent transaction) and executed immediately.

## **5. Time Limits by Request Type**

PanEuropean computes every deadline from receipt of the request. Because supervisory-authority practice on pausing the period while identity information is awaited differs among the five countries, PanEuropean does not rely on a pause (internal rule); the waiting time is recorded in the rights-request log for reporting. Example: an access request received by e-mail on 2 September 2026 — identity information requested on 3 September, received on 8 September, answer sent on 24 September 2026, within one month of receipt. If the customer has not supplied the requested information when the period is about to end, the customer is informed under Art. 12(4) that the request cannot be completed until identity is confirmed, and how to resubmit.

# **International Adaptation**

> **Guidance.** The GDPR is the baseline. The applicability map for Canada is in Document 7, Section 3.1. This box states what changes; it does not claim compliance with any jurisdiction's law.

* **United Kingdom (headline).** The UK GDPR as amended by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 provides that a controller responding to a subject access request is required only to make reasonable and proportionate searches, clarifies when the response period starts (including where identity information or a fee is awaited and where the controller asks the requester to clarify a request), and requires controllers to facilitate complaints made to them. Provisions commence in phases; record which apply at each review.
* **Switzerland (headline).** The revFADP grants a right of access with prescribed minimum content and a right to data portability; responses follow the time limits of the Data Protection Ordinance, and restrictions are those of the Act.
* **Canada — PIPEDA.** An organisation responds to an access request within 30 days of receipt; it may extend the time limit by a maximum of 30 further days where meeting it would unreasonably interfere with its activities or consultations are needed, or for the time necessary to convert the information into an alternative format, by notifying the individual of the extension, the reasons and the right to complain to the Privacy Commissioner. Failure to respond within the time limit is deemed a refusal. Access is provided at minimal or no cost, and the individual is told of the cost before it is incurred. Individuals may challenge the accuracy and completeness of the information and have it amended (Principle 4.9).
* **Canada — Québec (P-39.1).** The person in charge of the protection of personal information responds in writing within 30 days of receipt; failure to respond is deemed a refusal. On request, computerised personal information collected from the person is communicated in a structured, commonly used technological format, including to a person or body the person designates. A person may request de-indexation or cessation of dissemination in the conditions the Act sets. Where a decision is based exclusively on automated processing, the person is informed and, on request, told the personal information used, the reasons and principal factors and parameters that led to the decision, and of the right to have the information corrected; the person may submit observations to a member of staff who can review the decision.

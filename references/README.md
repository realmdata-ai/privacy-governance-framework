# Curated privacy references

A maintained entry point to laws, court decisions, regulator guidance and engineering standards relevant to the **Privacy Governance Framework (PGF)**.

The initial collection contains **39 selected entries**. It covers the EU/EEA and Canada, NIST and ENISA engineering references, selected UK/Swiss extensions, and an explicit watchlist. It is selective: US state/sector law, additional Canadian provincial and sector laws, and other jurisdictions require separate curation.

## How to use it

1. Select the jurisdiction, processing role and sector that actually apply.
2. Distinguish legislation and judgments from guidance, voluntary frameworks and professional standards.
3. Check the recorded status and version. Drafts and legislative proposals are in the watchlist and do not replace current obligations.
4. Open the official source, its applicable consolidated version and any commencement provisions. A working URL does not establish that a legal interpretation is current.
5. Follow the PGF document IDs as suggested entry points; they are **not a clause-level compliance crosswalk**.

Each source has a stable ID, official URL, topics, suggested PGF entry points, verification scope, last-check date and review-due date. Access restrictions and partial verification remain visible. A checked landing page is not a full legal review; every entry records this distinction.

References are links with short original curation notes. External texts are not included or relicensed. This catalogue does not certify a tool, an organisation or PGF as compliant.

## Planned extensions

Use these stable source IDs later to connect a risk taxonomy, harm scenarios, controls, evidence requirements and other curated data. Such mappings should be separate versioned collections referencing source IDs and, where justified, exact provisions. This first collection does not introduce a second risk-scoring model.

Maintainers may review the core collection quarterly and the watchlist monthly, and must check sources used in a release before freezing it. These are manual curation rules; no new scheduled task is created.
